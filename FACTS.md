# Calgary detached garage site: FACTS.md

Sourced facts file. Research date: 30 Sept 2026. Every figure below was read on the cited page that day (one exception, the
Airdrie page, was read from a Wayback Machine copy dated 14 Aug 2026 because airdrie.ca refused connections; see S61).
Site positioning: advertising that puts Calgary-area homeowners in touch with independent garage builders, who quote and
contract directly with the homeowner. **The site builds nothing, employs no crews, pulls no permits and holds no licence**
(no prepaid contracting licence, no builder licence, no trade certificate) and must never imply it does.

**Rules for using this file**

- This file is the ONLY source of numbers and factual claims the site may publish. If it is not here, it does not ship.
- `[S#]` points to the numbered source list in section 12. Quoted phrases are exact.
- Nothing is rounded. The site may round to 5 later. When two sources disagree, both are listed and a "USE" line says which one to publish.
- "Calgary price" means a price a firm states for Calgary. Any other place is labelled NOT CALGARY and may only be used as a comparison (area pages in section 10 may use their own town's figures).
- Builder prices come from each builder's own site. They are **what that builder advertises**, not a market survey and not our price. Always name the firm. GST treatment is noted per firm; "GST not stated" means the page does not say.
- City rules come from calgary.ca pages, which carry this disclaimer: "This information has no legal status and cannot be used as an official interpretation of the various bylaws, codes and regulations currently in effect." Where the bylaw text itself was read, the bylaw section is cited. [S1]
- Never publish a builder's claim about permits, fees or timelines when the City says something different. Those builder claims are listed below only so writers know to avoid them.

## 2. City of Calgary permits

### 2.1 When a permit is needed

- "A detached garage or similar building (shed, pergola, gazebo, greenhouse, arbour, workshop, playhouse or detached carport) is considered an accessory residential building in the Land Use Bylaw, and will require a building safety approval (building permit) if: It is greater than 10 m 2 (107 sq. ft.)" [S1]
- A garage attached to the house "is considered an addition and not a garage or similar structure." [S1]
- "Accessory residential buildings under 10 m² (107 sq. ft.) in area typically do not require a building permit, however, they must comply with the rules" on location and design. [S1]
- "If you are updating or building a garage that is not attached to your home, you need a Residential Improvement Project Permit." [S1][S2]
- "Temporary tent garages on residential properties do not require a building permit", but setback rules still apply ("tent garages are not considered maintenance free structures"). [S1]

### 2.2 When a development permit (planning approval) is also needed

A development permit is required, with "no guarantee of approval", when accessory residential building is discretionary in the district, when the design or location needs a relaxation of the Land Use Bylaw, or when building bigger or higher than the rules: [S1]
- "If the garage is higher than 4.6 meters (15’-1”)" [S1]
- "If the height from the parking surface to where the wall meets the roof truss is higher than 3 metres (9’-10”)" [S1]
- "If the garage is greater than 75 m 2 (807 sq. ft.)" [S1]
- "If the garage will be larger than the existing house" [S1]
- If the garage is closer than 1.0 m to the house: "If your building is closer than 1.0m you will need a development permit." [S1]
- "If you are proposing a rooftop deck or balcony, a development permit will always be required." [S1]
- Plain line safe to publish: a garage that meets every Land Use Bylaw rule needs a building permit (plus trade permits) but no development permit. [S1]

### 2.3 What the application needs

- Site plan items (Garage Permit Checklist): address; property lines; streets and back alleys/lane; outline of garage, home and other buildings; dimensions of house and garage; "Distance from property lines and house to proposed garage"; windows and doors; "Label direction of roof trusses"; "Length and location of driveway/apron"; height "from the parking surface to where the wall meets the roof and from parking surface to the peak"; exterior finish. "Tip: You can use a real property report as your site plan." [S5]
- Beam calculations or manufacturer's truss layouts: "only needed if the roof trusses are resting on the garage door." [S5]
- Maintenance Access Right-of-Way documents if one is registered on title. [S1][S5]
- "Drawings stamped with “not for construction”, “preliminary” or “for permit purposes only” are not acceptable." [S5]
- Development permit applications add: elevations of all four sides, a cross-section, an Abandoned Well Declaration and a Public Tree Disclosure Statement. [S1][S5]
- Who applies: "If you are hiring someone to do the work for you, they will need to apply for the permit." [S1] "If you’re a contractor or tradesperson hired to work on a new or existing property, it’s your responsibility to ensure the correct permits are in place before occupancy." [S1]
- Professional involvement: "An architect or professional engineer may be required to design and inspect your project. Refer to the current National Building Code – Alberta Edition, Division C, Section 2.4.2". [S1]
- Gas lines (older City list, updated 2016 February 16): "Provincial regulations prohibit the construction of a garage over a gas line. If faced with this conflict, either the garage must be moved at least 0.3m (1’-0”) from the gas line or the gas line must be sleeved with a gas tight sleeve." [S6]
- Unpaved lanes: a lane grade "can be established through a Development Site Servicing site visit." "This service is not mandatory." "Note: there is a fee for this service." [S1]
- Before digging: "contact click before you dig at 1-800-242-3447". [S1]

### 2.4 Trade permits (electrical, gas, plumbing)

- "All new garages will require a separate electrical permit for installing lights, outlets and the garage door." [S1] "If you are building a new detached garage, an electrical permit is always needed to install lights and to connect the overhead door." [S2]
- Gas: "A gas permit for a new gas line for a gas heater" is an example of a trade permit a detached garage may need. [S2]
- "Note: when an electrical contractor is doing the work only a Master Electrician can obtain an electrical permit." [S1][S2]
- Homeowner permits: a homeowner may apply for homeowner plumbing and electrical permits if they legally own the home, live in it or intend to (not a rental), and do the work themselves: "you cannot pull a permit on behalf of the individual or company doing the work." [S1]
- As a homeowner you can "Add a sub-panel to an existing main service, provided there is an existing main breaker." Homeowners may not "Install, alter or modify the main electrical service, including the main panel, main breaker or the meter base." [S1]
- Gas: a homeowner can pull a homeowner gas permit only if they hold a gas fitter journeyman certificate ("first-class or second-class gas fitter") and own and live in the home. [S1]

### 2.7 Inspections

- "In most cases, you only need a framing (rough-in) inspection to complete a residential improvement or renovation. In some cases, a safety code officer may require a second inspection." [S1]
- "The garage must be at lock-up stage, where all doors and windows are properly installed, with the building secure and the site safe." [S1]
- "The garage inspection can occur when the project is complete, or at the framing stage when doors and windows are installed." [S1]
- Electrical inspection: the safety codes officer "must have access to all areas where electrical work has been completed" and there must be "no exposed live wiring". [S1]
- Concealed wiring: "A homeowner’s permit cannot be issued for concealed wiring completed without proper permits." The owner must hire a licensed electrical contractor; "the inspector may ask for drywall to be removed". [S1]

### 2.8 Demolishing an old garage

- "In most cases, a demolition permit is not required for: Removing a detached residential garage". [S8]
- A demolition permit is only required for a detached garage over 10 m² if "The garage or accessory building is independently serviced, or" it "has the primary utility meters/connections for the residence." [S8]
- No demolition permit is needed if the garage "has no utility services, or" it "will be demolished and replaced with another garage or accessory building (the demolition of the existing structure is included in the permit for the new structure)." [S8]
- Where a demolition permit applies: "the applicant must submit the asbestos abatement form prior to starting demolition." [S8]
- Demolition permit fee basis: "$112 $1.44 / square metre" plus the 4% levy. [S3]

### 2.9 Real Property Report and compliance

- "You can use a Real Property Report (RPR) as your site plan." [S1]
- A Certificate of Compliance confirms that building locations comply with the Land Use Bylaw, "confirmed on a Real Property Report prepared by an Alberta land surveyor". Cost: "$189 per parcel" for single, semi-detached and duplex; "Initial Review: 1-2 days*". [S12]
- Common refusal reason: "Accessory Residential Buildings (such as Gazebos, sheds, garages) too close to the house". [S12]
- Not reviewed on a compliance: "Parcel coverage", "Building Height", and "Accessory Residential Buildings (sheds, pergolas etc.) less than 10m 2". [S12]
- "Altered Real Property Reports, unclear plans and plot plans will not be accepted." [S12]
- Rights-of-way: "Permanent structures are not permitted to be built in URWs. This includes, but is not limited to, any portion of a building, such as footings, walls or eaves." [S1]

## 3. Calgary Land Use Bylaw 1P2007

The online bylaw read on 30 Sept 2026 includes amending bylaws through 2026 (e.g. 9P2026, 26P2026). [S9] Section numbers below are from Part 5, Division 1 (low density residential districts).

### 3.1 Height (s. 345(6))

- "The height of an Accessory Residential Building must not exceed: a 4.6 metres , measured from the finished floor of the building ; b 3.0 metres at any eaveline , when measured from the finished floor of the building ; or c one storey". [S9]
- The attic allowed within that storey must be "accessed by a removable ladder", must not "have windows", must be "used by the occupants of the main residential building for placement of personal items", and has "a maximum height of 1.5 metres when measured from the attic floor to the underside of any rafter." [S9]
- The finished-floor 0.6 m limit does not apply to garages: "The finished floor of an Accessory Residential Building , other than a private garage , must not exceed 0.6 metres above grade ." (s. 346(1)) [S9]
- Conversions differ on City pages ("4.6m (15 ft)" in the rules section, "4.6 meters (15’-1”)" in the permit section). **USE metres.** [S1]

### 3.2 Size and coverage (s. 346(4))

- "The area of a parcel covered by all Accessory Residential Buildings located on a parcel : a must not exceed the lesser of: i the building coverage of the main residential building s ; or ii 75.0 square metres for each Dwelling Unit located on the parcel". [S9]
- Accessory buildings with "a cumulative gross floor area of 10.0 square metres or less" are left out of that calculation. [S9]
- Total parcel coverage (all buildings) by district, as the City's garage page tabulates it: R-C1L/R-C1Ls 40%; R-C1, R-C1s, R-C2, R-1, R-1s, R-MH, R-CG* 45%; R-C1N 45 or 50; R-1N 50, 60 or 45; R-2 45 or 50; R-2M 45, 50 or 60 depending on parcel size/width or dwelling type. "*Parcel coverage rules for R-CG change as density increases." [S1]
- The City's worked example: an R-C1 lot of 331.52 m² with house, covered deck, shed and a proposed 40.8 m² garage reaches 41.47%, under the 45% limit. [S1]
- Semi-detached garages: "The maximum size of each side of the garage is 75m 2 per single family home, per side of a semi-detached home and per rowhouse unit." [S1]

### 3.3 Setbacks (s. 345(1)–(4))

- "the minimum building setback for an Accessory Residential Building is: a 1.2 metres from a side or rear property line shared with a street ; or b 0.6 metres from a side or rear property line in all other cases." [S9]
- The 0.6 m setback "may be reduced to zero metres when: a the Accessory Residential Building is less than 10.0 square metres gross floor area ; b the wall of the Accessory Residential Building is constructed of maintenance-free materials and there is no overhang of eaves onto an adjacent parcel ; or c the owner of the adjacent parcel grants a 1.5 metre private maintenance easement" (registered on both titles, with "a 0.60 metre eave and footing encroachment easement"). [S9]
- City page examples of maintenance-free materials: "vinyl siding and stucco". [S1]
- "An Accessory Residential Building must not be located in the actual front setback area ." [S9]
- "A private garage on a laneless parcel may be located within the required 3.0 metre side setback area , except along the street side of a corner parcel ." [S9]

### 3.4 Distance from the house (s. 345(5))

- "The minimum distance between any façade of an Accessory Residential Building 10.0 square metres or more and a main residential building is 1.0 metres ." [S9] The City adds that this "Includes second floor cantilevers, bay windows etc." [S1]

### 3.5 Eaves and drainage

- "All roof drainage from an Accessory Residential Building must be discharged onto the parcel on which the building is located." (s. 346(5)) [S9]
- Building-code rule the City applies within 0.6 m of a side line: "Eave projections are not permitted within 0.45 m (1.5 ft.) of a side property line shared with an adjacent property (excluding rain trough)." "A non-vented soffit (underside of eave projection) is required for eaves within 1.2 m (3.9 ft.) of a property line shared with an adjacent property." [S1]
- General low-density rule: "Eaves may project a maximum of 0.6 metres into any side setback area ." (s. 337(3)) [S9]

### 3.6 Use limits (s. 346(2)–(3))

- "An Accessory Residential Building must not be used as a Dwelling Unit , unless a Backyard Suite has been approved." [S9]
- "An Accessory Residential Building must not have a balcony or rooftop deck ." [S9]

### 3.7 Driveways, lane access and parking (s. 341, parking stalls)

- Lane: "A driveway connecting a lane to a private garage must be a minimum of 0.60 metres in length along the intended direction of travel for vehicles, measured from the property line shared with the lane to the door of a private garage ." (s. 341(3)) [S9] The City page: "the garage apron must be 0.6m (1.9ft) in length when measured from the property line shared with the lane to the door of the garage". [S1]
- Street: a driveway from a street to a private garage must "be a minimum of 6.0 metres in length" and "contain a rectangular area measuring 6.0 metres in length and 3.0 metres in width." (s. 341(2)) [S9]
- Developed Area: a driveway accessing a street "must not be constructed, altered or replaced except where" the parcel is laneless, or 50.0 per cent or more parcels on the block face already have a street driveway, or a legally existing driveway is not being relocated or widened. (s. 341(6)) [S9]
- Parking: "At least one parking stall is required in most land use districts"; stalls "cannot be in tandem". Stall sizes: touching two barriers "at least 5.9 m x 3 m"; one barrier "5.9 m x 2.85 m"; no barriers "5.9 m x 2.5 m". Garage walls count as barriers. "Not all parking stalls need to be provided within a garage." [S1]
- Retaining walls: "A retaining wall must be less than 1.2 metres in height" measured as the bylaw sets out. (s. 342(1)) [S9]

## 5. Consumer protection and licensing

### 5.1 Prepaid contracting licence (Service Alberta)

- A prepaid contracting business "solicits, negotiates or concludes prepaid contracts in person at any place other than the sellers’ place of business" and "accepts money before all the work is done or the services are provided or both". [S18]
- "Prepaid contracts are for construction, maintenance, repairing, altering, adding to or improving private dwellings, or real property used in conjunction with a private dwelling such as landscaping services." [S18]
- To apply, a business provides business information, "a criminal record check or police information check", "a security", "a copy of standard contracts or agreements" and "payment of a licensing fee". [S18]
- Licensees "must be able to provide confirmation of licensing on request", including "a link to the Find a Licensed Business search tool". [S18]
- Persons selling for the business must carry an ID card with "the person’s name", "the business name, address and licence number". [S18]
- City of Calgary: a contractor business that "will accept deposits or progress payments from homeowners before all work is complete" — "You may need a Provincial Licence". [S28]

### 5.2 How a homeowner checks a licence

### 5.3 Cancellation rights (direct sales / prepaid contracts)

- Consumer Protection Act s. 27: "A consumer may, without any reason, cancel a direct sales contract at any time from the date the sales contract is entered into until, subject to the regulations, 10 days after the consumer receives a copy of the written sales contract." [S19]
- Section 28(2): cancellation "within one year" if "the supplier was required to be licensed under Part 10 and was not licensed" or the contract "does not include all the information required under section 35." [S19]
- Section 28(3): cancellation within one year if the supplier "does not begin the services within 30 days from the commencement date specified in the direct sales contract". [S19]
- Section 29(3): notice may be given "by any means, including, but not limited to, personal service, registered mail, courier or telecopier or by any other method, including orally, by which the consumer can provide evidence of the date". [S19]
- Section 31(2): "Within 15 days after a direct sales contract is cancelled, the supplier must refund to the consumer all money paid by the consumer". [S19]
- Statute version read: "Office Consolidation", "Current as of July 14, 2026". [S19]
- Consumer Bill of Rights: "When a written contract has been solicited, agreed upon or signed at your home or at another location away from the contractor’s normal place of business, the Consumer Protection Act allows you to cancel the contract up to 10 days after you receive your copy." [S20] Door-to-door conditions include "the purchase was for more than $25" and "you have one additional day to reconsider the purchase if the last day you can cancel falls on a Sunday or statutory holiday". [S20]
- Required contract contents (Service Alberta checklist, "Published: January 2019") include the buyer's and seller's names and addresses, "a statement of cancellation rights", "the itemized prices", "the total amount", "the terms of payment", the commencement and completion dates, and signatures. [S21]
- Scope note: these rights apply to contracts "negotiated or concluded in person at a place other than the supplier’s place of business". [S19] A contract signed at the builder's office is not a direct sales contract under that definition.

- Carpenter does not appear on Alberta's compulsory certification trades list (derived from reading the list). [S27]

- Alberta residential builder licence: "All residential builders in Alberta must have a licence to obtain permits for constructing new homes." [S26] NOT FOUND: any Alberta rule requiring a builder licence for a detached garage.

### 5.5 New home warranty and detached garages

- Construction Performance Guide for New Home Warranty in Alberta (2015-09-01): "non-residential detached structures such as garages, garden sheds or other outbuildings (certain exceptions apply to condominium common property) may also be excluded." and "Detached garages may be excluded from coverage." [S24]
- Home Warranty Insurance Regulation s. 6(a): a warranty provider may exclude "any non-residential use area and any construction or reconstruction associated with a non-residential use area". [S25]
- Alberta: "When a structure not originally built as a dwelling (such as a, garage, granary or church) is to be converted into a residential home, the requirements of the New Home Buyer Protection Act must be met for building permits to be issued." [S23]
- Safe line: a new detached garage built on its own for an existing home is not a new home, and the provincial new home warranty rules let warranty providers exclude detached garages. Do not say a garage "is covered" or "is not covered" by any warranty. [S24][S25]
