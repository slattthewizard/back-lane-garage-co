# Development Permit for a Garage in Calgary: The Six Triggers, the Fee and the Wait

Most detached garages in Calgary need a building permit and nothing more. A development permit is a second, slower approval that applies only when the garage steps outside what the Land Use Bylaw allows. The difference is about $550 and roughly three months, so it is worth knowing which side of the line your design sits on before you pay for drawings.

This page lists what triggers a development permit, what the application adds, what it costs and how long the City says it takes. City pages on this subject say they have no legal status and cannot be used as an official interpretation of the bylaws. Confirm your own garage with the City.

## Building permit or development permit?

Two different approvals can apply to one garage.

A building permit checks that the garage meets the safety codes. The City says a detached garage over 10 m² (107 sq ft) needs one, and it lists a review time of approximately 7 days. The 2026 fee for a new garage is $333.84.

A development permit checks the garage against planning rules in the Land Use Bylaw: where it sits, how big it is and how high. The City says a garage that meets every Land Use Bylaw rule needs a building permit, plus trade permits such as electrical, but no development permit.

The City also says a detached garage always needs a separate electrical permit for lights, outlets and the overhead door. For the general picture of who applies and what the City asks for, see [the Calgary garage permit guide](/blog/calgary-garage-permit/).

## The six triggers

The City lists these situations where a development permit is required. Any one is enough.

1. The garage is higher than 4.6 m (15 ft 1 in).
2. The height from the parking surface to where the wall meets the roof truss is more than 3 m (9 ft 10 in).
3. The garage is greater than 75 m² (807 sq ft).
4. The garage will be larger than the existing house.
5. The garage is closer than 1.0 m to the house. The City says this includes cantilevers and bay windows on the house.
6. A rooftop deck or balcony is proposed. The City says a development permit is always required for these.

On top of the six, a development permit is also needed when accessory residential building is a discretionary use in your district, or when the design or location needs a relaxation of the bylaw, for example a setback smaller than the minimum. The City adds that a development permit comes with no guarantee of approval.

The 75 m² figure is a ceiling on one garage. All accessory buildings on the parcel together must not exceed the lesser of the house's building coverage or 75 m² per dwelling unit. A small house on a lot can therefore cap the garage below 75 m². Accessory buildings with a combined floor area of 10 m² or less are left out of that calculation. Total parcel coverage limits by district also apply.

## Relaxations and discretionary use

A relaxation is a request to the City to approve a design or location that does not meet the bylaw as written. The City's fee schedule has a line for exactly this: proposed structures that do not meet all rules of Land Use Bylaw 1P2007.

A discretionary use works differently. The City says a development permit is required when an accessory residential building is a discretionary use in the district. The same building can be a permitted use in one district and a discretionary one in another, so check your parcel's district on the City's site before you design anything. It decides which path you are on from the start.

## What the development permit application adds

The building permit application needs a site plan showing property lines, streets and lane, the outline of the house and garage, distances from the garage to the property lines and the house, windows and doors, the direction of roof trusses, the driveway or apron, and heights from the parking surface to where the wall meets the roof and to the peak. The City says a Real Property Report can serve as the site plan. If a Maintenance Access Right-of-Way is registered on title, those documents go in too.

A development permit application adds:

- elevations of all four sides of the garage
- a cross-section
- an Abandoned Well Declaration
- a Public Tree Disclosure Statement

The City says drawings stamped "not for construction", "preliminary" or "for permit purposes only" are not acceptable. It also says an application may be refused if, within 90 days of receipt, adequate information and documentation is not supplied. An incomplete package costs you time even before the review starts.

## The $550 fee, broken down

The City's 2026 Planning Applications Fee Schedule prices a development permit for a proposed structure that does not meet all the rules at $550. That is made up of:

| Part | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base fee | $365 |
| Development completion inspection | $155 |
| Advertising | $30 |
| Total | $550 |

This is separate from the building permit. As plain arithmetic, a garage needing both pays $550 plus $333.84, or $883.84, before electrical and other trade permits. A homeowner electrical permit is listed at $116.50 and a contractor trade permit starts at the same minimum with a variable amount on top. Fees are paid in full at application, and the City charges double the permit fee if work begins before the permit is issued.

Some builders publish permit figures that differ from the City's schedule. Use the City's numbers, and ask any firm quoting you whether its price includes the permit fee or leaves it to you. Venkor says City permits and design drawings are included in its quote, and Corsac says the garage electrical permit and inspection are included on its detached garages.

## How long it takes: about 10 to 12 weeks, against about 7 days

The City's page gives these timelines for a new garage:

| Permit | City's stated time |
|---|---|
| Building permit | Approximately 7 days |
| Development permit | 10 to 12 weeks |

The development permit figure is made up of 60 days to a decision plus a 21 day advertisement and appeal period. The City says these timelines are not a guarantee and depend on application volumes, how complete the application is and how quickly the applicant answers questions. Its page also said it was experiencing higher than seasonal application volumes at the time it was read.

The difference matters for planning. If you want the garage built in summer, a 10 to 12 week approval means the application has to go in well before the building season. Hammerhead Construction says more projects are completed in the summer months, so demand for labour can be higher then. Quoting in winter gives more time. ## No guarantee of approval

A relaxation request asks the City to make an exception, so the answer is not automatic. The City states plainly that there is no guarantee of approval. The fee includes an advertising amount and the timeline includes an advertisement and appeal period, so the application is not a private matter between you and the City.

A refusal after paying $550 and waiting three months is the real cost of an optimistic design. If a garage can be redesigned to fit within the rules, that is usually the quicker route.

## How to design under the triggers

Because each trigger is a number, you can check a design against all six before you draw anything.

- Height. Keep the garage at or below 4.6 m, measured from the finished floor, with no more than 3.0 m at any eaveline and one storey. Keep the wall-to-truss height measured from the parking surface at or below 3 m. A taller door or ceiling may push this over, so ask the builder to state the measurement.
- Size. Stay at or below 75 m², and below the footprint of the house. Common double sizes sit well under: a 24x24 is about 53.5 m².
- Distance from the house. Keep at least 1.0 m between the garage and the nearest part of the house, including cantilevers. The Certificate of Compliance page lists a garage too close to the house as a common refusal reason.
- Setbacks. Stay within the standard minimums so no relaxation is needed. The side and rear minimums are 0.6 m, or 1.2 m where the line is shared with a street.
- Roof. No deck or balcony on an accessory building.

A garage that stays inside every number needs no development permit. If you want something taller or wider than those numbers, the permit is the price of that choice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a detached garage always need a development permit in Calgary?

No. A garage that meets every Land Use Bylaw rule needs a building permit and trade permits but no development permit. A development permit applies when the design exceeds a limit, needs a relaxation or the use is discretionary in the district.

### How much is a development permit for a garage in Calgary?

The City's 2026 fee for a proposed structure that does not meet all the bylaw rules is $550. That is $365 base, $155 for the development completion inspection and $30 for advertising. The building permit is a separate $333.84.

### How long does a garage development permit take?

The City lists 10 to 12 weeks, made up of 60 days to a decision and a 21 day advertisement and appeal period. A building permit is listed at approximately 7 days. The City says these times are not a guarantee.

### Can the City refuse a garage development permit?

Yes. The City says there is no guarantee of approval. An application may also be refused if adequate information is not supplied within 90 days of receipt.
