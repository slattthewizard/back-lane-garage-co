# How Long to Build a Garage in Calgary: A Step-by-Step Timeline

The honest answer is that the building takes a few weeks and everything around it takes longer. How long to build a garage depends on whether the City needs a development permit, how complete your application is, and how soon your builder can start. This page walks through the steps in order, with the City's stated times and what Calgary builders say about their own.

None of these numbers is a promise. If you want to talk through your situation, [call (403) 555-0162](tel:+14035550162). Independent Calgary garage builders quote and contract with you directly.

## The short version

- A building permit for a new garage is listed by the City at approximately 7 days.
- A development permit, if your design needs one, is listed at 10 to 12 weeks.
- The builders we read say the construction itself takes roughly 2 to 8 weeks, depending on the firm and what is being counted.

The City says its timelines are not a guarantee and depend on volumes, application completeness, and how quickly the applicant answers requests for more information. City pages also say they have no legal status, so confirm requirements with the City.

## Step 1: collect written quotes

Start with quotes from independent builders, using the same size, walls, door and electrical on each. Venkor Group says city permits and design drawings are included in its quote, and Corsac Construction says the garage electrical permit and inspection are included on its detached garages. Others may handle it differently, so ask who applies for the permit and when.

The City says that if you hire someone to do the work, they need to apply for the permit. The application is the first step that depends on someone else, so ask when your builder would submit and what they need from you first.

The [cost guide](/blog/cost-to-build-a-garage-calgary/) covers what to compare in a quote.

## Step 2: get a Real Property Report or site plan

The City's application needs a site plan showing the property lines, the garage, the house and the distances between them. The City says you can use a Real Property Report (RPR) as your site plan. If you have one from when you bought the house, check that it is accurate and shows what is on the lot now.

The City also offers a Certificate of Compliance, which confirms that building locations comply with the Land Use Bylaw using an RPR prepared by an Alberta land surveyor. It costs $189 per parcel for a single-family home, with an initial review listed at 1 to 2 days. It is a separate service from the building permit, so ask your builder whether it is useful in your case.

The City says altered Real Property Reports, unclear plans and plot plans will not be accepted, so clean documents save time.

## Step 3: building permit, approximately 7 days

A detached garage over 10 m2 (107 sq ft) needs a building permit. The City lists the review of a new garage application at approximately 7 days, and the 2026 fee at $333.84 including the Safety Codes Council fee. The timeline assumes a complete application. The City says it may refuse an application if adequate information and documentation is not supplied within 90 days of receipt, so an application that sits waiting for a missing drawing can fall apart.

The City's page also said it was experiencing higher than seasonal application volumes and apologized for delays. Treat 7 days as the City's stated figure, not a date you can plan a crew around.

The City lists building permit expiry at 180 days from issuance, so agree a start date with your builder before it is issued. Contractor trade permits and homeowner permits run to 330 days.

See the [Calgary garage permit guide](/blog/calgary-garage-permit/) for the full list of what the City asks for.

## Step 4: development permit, 10 to 12 weeks, only if triggered

A development permit is a separate approval and it is the biggest swing in the timeline. The City says it is needed when the garage needs a relaxation of the Land Use Bylaw or when you build bigger or higher than the rules allow. Examples the City lists:

- higher than 4.6 metres
- more than 3 metres from the parking surface to where the wall meets the roof truss
- larger than 75 m2 (807 sq ft)
- larger than the existing house
- closer than 1.0 metre to the house

The City lists 60 days to a decision, plus a 21-day advertisement and appeal period, which it puts at 10 to 12 weeks. There is no guarantee of approval. A garage that meets every Land Use Bylaw rule needs a building permit and trade permits but no development permit. The 2026 development permit fee for a proposed structure that does not meet all the rules is $550.

If you are close to one of those lines, ask your builder to design just under it. Wall height counts from the parking surface to where the wall meets the truss, so tell the builder if you want a tall door.

## Step 5: electrical and other trade permits

All new garages need a separate electrical permit for lights, outlets and connecting the garage door. If you add a gas heater, a gas permit is needed too. When an electrical contractor does the work, only a Master Electrician can obtain the permit. Ask whether your builder arranges this and whether it is in the quote.

## Step 6: click before you dig

Before any digging, the City says to contact Click Before You Dig at 1-800-242-3447. The City page lists the call among the steps before work starts. Ask your builder to confirm who makes it and when.

## Step 8: inspections

The City says that in most cases you only need a framing (rough-in) inspection for a garage, and a safety codes officer may require a second one. For the garage inspection the building must be at lock-up stage, with all doors and windows installed and the site safe. It can happen at framing stage when doors and windows are in, or when the project is complete.

For electrical, the safety codes officer must have access to all areas where electrical work has been done, and there must be no exposed live wiring. Confirm who will book the inspections. Re-inspections are charged, and the 2026 City fee is $327 including GST.

A good schedule has an inspection date in it. Ask each builder when they expect to call for it.

## What slows a garage down

From the City's side: an incomplete application, a development permit, and slow replies to City questions. From the builder's side: weather, concrete in cold months, and a full calendar in summer. From your side: changing the design after you apply, and not having the site plan ready.

Arithmetic on the published figures: with no development permit, about 7 days for the building permit and 3 to 5 weeks of construction after it comes to roughly one to two months from a complete application. With a development permit, add 10 to 12 weeks to the front. These are sums of stated figures, not forecasts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a building permit for a garage take in Calgary?

The City lists approximately 7 days for a new garage building permit. It says timelines are not a guarantee and depend on volumes, application completeness and how fast the applicant answers questions. It has also noted higher than seasonal volumes.

### How long does a development permit take?

The City lists 60 days to a decision plus a 21-day advertisement and appeal period, which it puts at 10 to 12 weeks. A development permit is only needed if the design does not meet every Land Use Bylaw rule, for example if it is higher than 4.6 metres.

### How long does the garage itself take to build?

Builders we read give a few weeks. Sahi Contracting says 3 to 5 weeks once permits are in hand, and Falcon Contracting says 4 to 8 weeks from excavation to inspection. Ask your builder for a written schedule.

### How long is a garage building permit valid?

The City lists 180 days from issuance for a building permit. Confirm your builder can start inside that window before the permit is issued.
