# Building a Hail Resistant Garage in Calgary

A detached garage sits in the open, usually at the back of the lot, with a large roof and walls and no house to shelter it. In a city that gets hailstorms, the roof and siding choices you make before construction are the ones that matter. This page covers what the Insurance Bureau of Canada (IBC) says about Calgary storms and what to ask when you collect quotes for a hail resistant garage.

## Why Calgary builders talk about hail

IBC is Canada's insurance industry association. It has published figures on the large hailstorms that hit Calgary and the surrounding area. These are insured losses across homes, businesses and vehicles, not figures for garages.

- 13 June 2020: the hail, rain and windstorm that hit Calgary, Airdrie, Rocky View County and the surrounding area caused almost $1.2 billion in insured damages, according to Catastrophe Indices and Quantification Inc. (CatIQ), as IBC reported in July 2020.
- 2021: IBC later referred to the 2020 and 2021 hailstorms as causing over $1.2 billion and $700 million in insured damage, respectively.
- 5 August 2024: IBC's first estimate was nearly $2.8 billion. Its update a year later says the storm caused over $3.2 billion in insured losses and was the costliest hailstorm, and second costliest natural disaster, in Canadian history. IBC also said almost one in five homes in Calgary was impacted and that insurers were working to process over 130,000 claims.
- 13 July 2025: a further storm caused close to $92 million in insured damages. IBC said insured damage from hailstorms in Alberta over the past five years now stands at $6 billion, and that many areas of Calgary sit within Canada's "Hail Alley".

You can read the 2025 update on the [IBC site](https://www.ibc.ca/news-insights/news/latest-hailstorm-in-calgary-causes-92-million-in-insured-damage).

We looked for IBC, insurer or City figures for hail damage to garages or garage doors specifically, for 2020 or 2024, and did not find any. None of the numbers above tell you how likely a particular garage is to be damaged. They tell you that hail is a repeat event in this region, which is why the roof and siding specification deserves a line in your quote.

## What IBC recommends for new construction

IBC's recommendations after the 2025 storm include a call to mandate the use of hail-resistant roofing and siding for all new construction in high-risk areas. That is an insurance-industry recommendation, not a Calgary building rule. Nothing we found from the City of Calgary requires hail-resistant roofing on a detached garage.

IBC also called on governments to revive Calgary's Resilient Roofing Rebate program. We found no sign that it is open now, so do not budget for a rebate. The City launched the rebate in June 2021 with a $3,000 roofing rebate for Class 4 impact-resistant roofing, aimed at owners of single-family, semi-detached or duplex homes in good standing on their property tax roll. Its first year ended on 31 May 2022 or when funding was fully allocated. We did not find whether it covered detached garage roofs, and we did not find a City statement that it is open now. If you hear about a rebate from a builder, ask them to show it to you in writing from the City.

## What builders say about hail

Builders do not publish hail test results for their garage packages. What we found is one statement. Corsac Construction says on its garage packages page that James Hardie siding can be selected for its authentic appearance, durability, and added protection to the garage against Calgary's harsh weather conditions, including hail. That is the builder's statement, not a City standard or an independent test.

Most advertised packages are described in more general terms. Venkor Group lists vinyl siding to match the main dwelling. Calgary Garage Builders Ltd lists vinyl siding in its package. If hail resistance matters to you, the standard package is a starting point and the siding and roofing become line items to price.

## Roof and siding questions to put in writing

Ask each builder the same things and get the answers on the quote.

- What roofing product is in the price, and is it an impact-resistant product? The City's old rebate used the term Class 4 impact-resistant roofing, so ask whether the shingle on offer carries that rating.
- What siding is in the price (vinyl, or an upgrade such as James Hardie)?
- What does the hail-resistant option add to the price compared with the standard package?
- Is the roofing and siding warranty from the manufacturer, the builder, or both, and what does it say about hail?
- Who installs it, and does the installer's work have a separate warranty?

Do not accept "hail rated" with no product name. A product name and rating can be checked; a phrase cannot.

## What this does and does not tell you about insurance

IBC says hail and wind damage are covered by standard home and business insurance and by comprehensive auto insurance policies. That is a general statement from the industry association.

We did not find a primary source that explains how Alberta home policies treat detached garages, and we are not giving advice on policies or claims here. How your own policy treats the garage, and what it asks of you if something happens, is a question for your insurer or broker, and it is worth asking before you build.

## Reading the storm figures fairly

A few plain observations on the numbers, labelled as our arithmetic. The 2024 figure of over $3.2 billion is a little under three times the almost $1.2 billion IBC reported for 2020. IBC's separate five-year Alberta total of $6 billion covers hailstorms across the province, so it should not be added to the Calgary storm figures.

None of this predicts what happens to a given roof in a given year. What it supports is a narrower point: hail has been costly in Calgary more than once in five years, and a detached garage is one more roof and set of walls exposed to it. That is a reason to ask about the specification, not a reason to pay for whatever a builder labels hail resistant.

## Permit and design points that still apply

Choosing hail-resistant materials does not change the City's approval path. A garage over 10 m2 (107 sq ft) needs a building permit. The City lists the building permit at approximately 7 days, with no guarantee, and a development permit at 10 to 12 weeks when one is needed. City pages carry the note that they have no legal status, so confirm with the City.

Wall and roof height still matter. The Land Use Bylaw limits an accessory building to 4.6 metres in height, and the City asks for a development permit if the height from the parking surface to where the wall meets the roof truss is higher than 3 metres (9 ft 10 in). Ask your builder to confirm the design stays inside those lines before you settle the specification.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there a hail-proof garage?

We did not find a builder or City source that promises one. Corsac Construction says James Hardie siding gives added protection against Calgary's harsh weather, including hail, and the City's old rebate used Class 4 impact-resistant roofing as its standard. Ask for the specific product and rating in writing.

### Does Calgary require hail-resistant roofing on a detached garage?

We did not find a City requirement. IBC recommends mandating hail-resistant roofing and siding for all new construction in high-risk areas, but that is a recommendation to governments, not a rule. Confirm any requirement with the City.

### Is the Calgary roofing rebate still available?

The City launched the Resilient Roofing Rebate in 2021 with a $3,000 rebate for Class 4 impact-resistant roofing, and its first year ended on 31 May 2022 or when funding ran out. In 2025 IBC called on governments to revive it. We did not find a current offer and did not find that it covered garages.

### How much insured damage has hail caused in Calgary?

IBC reports almost $1.2 billion for the 13 June 2020 storm and over $3.2 billion for the 5 August 2024 storm. It also says insured hail damage in Alberta over the five years to mid-2025 stands at $6 billion. These are insured losses across all property, not figures for garages.
