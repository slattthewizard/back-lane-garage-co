# Garage Attic Storage in Calgary: What the Bylaw Allows

The ceiling of a detached garage is the cheapest storage space most owners never use. Bins of winter tires, camping gear and holiday boxes all fit above the rafters if someone builds a floor for them. Calgary allows that, but only as an attic, and the City defines the attic narrowly.

## The rule: one storey, with an attic inside it

The bylaw section that governs height for accessory residential buildings, a category that includes detached garages, sets three limits. A garage must not exceed:

- 4.6 metres, measured from the finished floor of the building.
- 3.0 metres at any eaveline, measured from the finished floor.
- One storey.

The attic is allowed inside that one storey, not on top of it. The bylaw gives four conditions for the attic space:

- It is accessed by a removable ladder.
- It has no windows.
- It is used by the occupants of the main residential building for placement of personal items.
- It has a maximum height of 1.5 metres, measured from the attic floor to the underside of any rafter.

The wording is specific. It does not describe a finished room with a staircase, a window for light or a place to work. It describes a space you reach with a ladder you can take away, holding things that belong to the people who live in the house.

## What each condition means in practice

A removable ladder rules out a fixed staircase. A pull-down or lift-away ladder is the kind of access the wording describes. If a builder proposes a permanent stair to the upper space, ask where the City's allowance comes from, because a fixed stair is not what the bylaw text describes.

No windows is a flat prohibition, so a window that gives the attic daylight is not allowed under this wording. If a drawing shows a window in the attic space, ask the builder which section of the bylaw it relies on.

Personal items of the main house's occupants is a use restriction. It describes storage for the household that lives in the house. It does not describe using the space as a room.

The 1.5 metre maximum is measured from the attic floor to the underside of any rafter. That is the clear height at the tallest point of the attic, and it limits what kind of roof framing makes sense. A steep roof does not create more attic height than the bylaw allows, because the cap applies to the measurement as written.

## Height limits and when a development permit appears

The City's garage page says a development permit, a separate planning approval with no guarantee of approval, is needed if the garage is higher than 4.6 metres, or if the height from the parking surface to where the wall meets the roof truss is more than 3 metres (9 feet 10 inches). Building bigger than the rules moves you out of the building-permit-only route.

Compare that with the wall heights builders advertise. Venkor Group lists 9.5 foot walls in its packages and Corsac Construction lists 8 foot walls, with a 9 foot ceiling as an add-on. Converting 9.5 feet to metres gives about 2.9 metres, which is under the City's 3 metre figure. That is arithmetic only. The City measures from the parking surface to the point where the wall meets the truss, so ask the builder to show you that exact dimension on the drawing.

The cost of going beyond the limits is time and money. The City's development permit fee for proposed structures that do not meet all the Land Use Bylaw rules is $550, and its stated timeline is 10 to 12 weeks, against approximately 7 days for a building permit alone. The City says its timelines are not a guarantee. A garage that meets every rule needs a building permit and trade permits, and no development permit. The full process is in [the Calgary garage permit guide](/blog/calgary-garage-permit/).

## What an attic cannot be

Several things people ask for are ruled out by the bylaw text.

A second storey. The limit is one storey, and the attic sits within it. A full upper floor does not fit.

A balcony or rooftop deck. The bylaw says an accessory residential building must not have either. The City's garage page adds that if you propose a rooftop deck or balcony, a development permit will always be required, so the City treats it as an application, not an automatic refusal. The bylaw wording itself is a flat prohibition, so confirm with the City before you plan one.

A dwelling. An accessory residential building must not be used as a dwelling unit unless a backyard suite has been approved. An attic with a bed in it is not a loophole. A garage with a suite is a backyard suite, and suites require different approvals and a different process from a garage. This site does not cover them. See the City's backyard suite page for that process.

## If your garage is outside Calgary

The rules above are Calgary's. Neighbouring municipalities set their own, and some say less about attics than the City does.

Rocky View County's building permit checklist for residential accessory buildings says garages with a second floor or mezzanine require a site-specific engineered foundation design. Pre-engineered slab-on-grade designs are offered only for eligible single-storey detached garages. So the County treats upper-floor space as a trigger for engineering that a plain single-storey garage avoids. The checklist also says all truss profiles and layouts must be stamped by a licensed Alberta engineer. Rocky View's accessory building size, height and setback limits vary by district, so check yours. See [garage builders in Rocky View County](/garage-builders-rocky-view-county/) for the area.

Airdrie states a height limit of 5 metres from ground to peak, or the height of the principal dwelling, whichever is smaller. That is a different measurement from Calgary's 4.6 metres from the finished floor, so a design drawn for one city does not carry over to the other.

## Slabs, trusses and the attic

Storage overhead is a load on the building, and several choices at the bottom and top of the garage connect to it.

The City's slab rule is about floor area, not attic use: a slab larger than 55 square metres without a concrete foundation 1.2 metres below grade needs a slab designed by a professional engineer. The City's regulation bulletin allows a garage of less than 55 square metres and not more than one storey to sit on a 100 millimetre concrete floor slab. Whether your attic storage changes what a builder wants under the slab is a question for that builder and, if needed, for an engineer. The sources this site reads do not say.

Roof trusses matter more. The City's checklist asks for the direction the trusses run, and it asks for beam calculations or a truss manufacturer's layout where trusses rest on the garage door. If you want usable attic space, the truss design decides how much of it you get. Ask for the truss design in writing, and ask who is stamping it. Rocky View says Alberta engineer stamps are required there, and Airdrie says all truss specifications need an engineer's seal as of 1 March 2026. The sources read for this site do not include a Calgary statement on that, so ask the City or your builder.

Attic storage is not named in the package inclusions this site read. Garage package pages from Venkor, Corsac and Calgary Garage Builders Ltd list the slab, framing, siding, door and electrical. ## Questions to ask each builder

- What is the clear height at the tallest point of the attic, measured from the attic floor to the underside of the rafter?
- How will the attic be reached, and is the ladder removable?
- Does the drawing show any window in the attic space?
- What is the height from the parking surface to where the wall meets the truss?
- Who designs and stamps the trusses, and is that in the price?
- Is a development permit needed for this design, and who pays the $550 fee if it is?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I put a loft or second floor in a detached garage in Calgary?

The bylaw limits an accessory residential building to one storey, with a maximum height of 4.6 metres and 3.0 metres at the eaveline. An attic is allowed inside that storey if it meets four conditions, including a removable ladder, no windows and a maximum height of 1.5 metres. A full second floor does not fit the wording.

### Can I put windows in a garage attic?

No. The bylaw says the attic must not have windows. It also limits the use to personal items of the people who live in the main house.

### Can I finish the attic as a room or suite?

Not under the garage rules. An accessory building cannot be used as a dwelling unit unless a backyard suite has been approved, and suites go through different permits. Ask the City about that process.

### What does Rocky View County require for a garage with an upper floor?

Its checklist says garages with a second floor or mezzanine require a site-specific engineered foundation design. Pre-engineered slab designs apply only to eligible single-storey garages.
