## Do You Need a Garage Permit in Calgary? The 10 m2 Rule

The City calls a detached garage an "accessory residential building". It needs a building permit if it is greater than 10 m2 (107 sq ft). A 12x20 single garage is 240 sq ft, so it clears that line easily. An attached garage is handled differently: the City counts it as an addition to the house, not as a garage.

The building permit checks the structure against the building code. A development permit checks the garage against the Land Use Bylaw, or asks to break one of its rules. A garage that meets every Land Use Bylaw rule needs a building permit and trade permits but no development permit. That is the ordinary, quick path.

One rule decides who does the paperwork. The City says that if you hire someone to do the work, they will need to apply for the permit. So the builder quoting your job should tell you, in writing, who applies and whether the permit cost sits inside the quote.

## Building Permit vs Development Permit for a Calgary Garage

A new garage building permit is $321 plus a 4% Safety Codes Council fee, which comes to $333.84 in 2026. The City lists the review time as approximately 7 days, though it also notes higher-than-seasonal volumes and possible delays. Treat 7 days as the City's estimate, not a promise.

The development permit is the slow one. You need it if any of these is true:

- The garage is higher than 4.6 m.
- The wall-to-truss height from the parking surface is over 3 m.
- The garage is over 75 m2 (807 sq ft).
- The garage is larger than the house.
- The garage is closer than 1.0 m to the house.
- You want any relaxation of a bylaw rule.
- There is a rooftop deck or balcony, which the bylaw does not allow on a garage anyway.

A development permit for a relaxation is $550 in 2026, and the City lists 10 to 12 weeks for a decision (60 days to decide plus a 21-day appeal period). For a tall or oversized garage, the real cost is about a quarter of a year on the calendar, so a summer build needs that question settled in winter.

| Item | What the City says | 2026 fee or timing |
|---|---|---|
| Building permit, new detached garage | Required over 10 m2 (107 sq ft) | $321 + 4% = $333.84; about 7 days |
| Development permit | Only if a bylaw trigger applies | $550 for a relaxation; 10 to 12 weeks |
| Electrical permit | Separate permit for lights, outlets and the door | Homeowner permit $116.50 for your own work |
| Gas permit | Needed for a gas heater | Homeowners only if a certified gas fitter |
| Starting before the permit | Not allowed | Charged double the permit fee |
| Inspection | Usually one framing (rough-in) inspection | At lock-up, doors and windows installed |

Every new garage needs its own electrical permit, even if the only load is a light and a door opener. A homeowner electrical permit only covers work you do yourself in the home you live in, so a hired builder's electrician has to be a Master Electrician pulling the permit.

## What the City Checks on Your Garage Plans

The site plan shows property lines, the lane, the garage and house outlines, the distance from each property line and from the house, windows and doors, and the apron. You also mark the direction of the roof trusses and give the wall height and peak height measured from the parking surface.

A Real Property Report can serve as the site plan, which saves a step. Drawings marked "preliminary" or "for permit purposes only" are not accepted. That matters if a builder hands you a stock drawing to save time.

Then come the Land Use Bylaw 1P2007 limits. Sections 341, 345 and 346 cover accessory buildings, and neither the 2024 citywide rezoning nor its 2026 repeal changed them. These are the numbers reviewers check:

- **Height:** 4.6 m maximum from the finished floor, 3.0 m at any eaveline, one storey. An attic is allowed only for storage, with a removable ladder, no windows and a maximum 1.5 m height.
- **Size:** all accessory buildings together must not exceed the lesser of the house's building coverage or 75 m2 per dwelling unit. Parcel coverage limits also apply by district, for example 45% in R-C1 and R-C2.
- **Setbacks:** 0.6 m from side and rear lines, and 1.2 m where the line is shared with a street. No garage in the front setback. At least 1.0 m between garage and house. A 0 m side setback is possible only with maintenance-free walls and no eave overhang, or a registered 1.5 m maintenance easement.
- **Eaves and drainage:** no eaves within 0.45 m of a shared side line, and roof drainage must stay on your lot.
- **Fire protection:** 5/8" Type X drywall, floor to ceiling, on interior walls within 0.6 m of a side property line.
- **Lane apron:** at least 0.6 m from the lane property line to the garage door.

If your design bends any of these, that is a relaxation, and it means a development permit.

## Garage Permit Calgary Rules Change Outside the City Limits

Calgary's numbers do not travel. If your lot is in Airdrie, Cochrane, Okotoks, Chestermere or Rocky View County, use that municipality's rules, not the City's.

- **Airdrie:** a permit for every garage over 10 m2, a maximum of 5 m to the peak or the house height (whichever is smaller), no windows, doors or vents within 1.2 m of a property line, and a 45-minute fire rating within 0.6 m. A Real Property Report is required, and since 1 March 2026 roof truss designs need an Alberta engineer's seal.
- **Cochrane:** no development permit up to 53.5 m2 and 4.6 m tall. Side and rear setbacks are 0.6 m, rising to 1.2 m at the rear for lane-access garages, and 1.5 m from the house. The building permit is $0.25 per sq ft with a $90 minimum, plus 4%.
- **Okotoks:** a building permit is required and the garage may not be taller than the house. A development permit is needed outside the Traditional Neighbourhood district or when placement standards are not met. An engineer or architect is needed over 55 m2. Review takes 5 to 10 business days.
- **Chestermere:** a "garage" is 10 to 50 m2. Anything bigger becomes "Accessory Building, Other", which is discretionary in R-1. In R-1, the side setback is 0.6 m, the rear is 1.0 m and the maximum height is 4.5 m. Fees are not published, so ask the City.
- **Rocky View County:** a building permit over 10 m2, and no development permit for garages that comply with the Land Use Bylaw. Building permit fees are $0.30 per sq ft under 55 m2 unfinished and $0.40 over, plus 4%. Truss layouts must be engineer-stamped. Size and height limits vary by district, so check the County for your parcel.

For Calgary itself, no City page confirms a truss-stamp requirement, so ask your builder.

## What a Builder Should Handle, and What You Should Confirm

Quotes treat permits in very different ways. Venkor Group says City permits and design drawings are included in its quote. Corsac lists "Garage electrical permit and inspection included" for its packages. Another builder may quote the garage alone and leave the permits to you. Those are not the same price.

Ask each builder for the following in writing:

1. Who applies for the building permit, and whether the fee is in the price.
2. Who pulls the electrical permit, and that it is a Master Electrician.
3. Whether a gas permit is needed, and whether a certified gas fitter pulls it.
4. Whether the plan is likely to trigger a development permit, and what that does to the schedule.
5. Whether the drawings are final, not marked "preliminary".

Then check the builder. Alberta requires a prepaid contractor licence for a builder that sells at your home and takes money before the work is done. Service Alberta has a "Find out if a business is licensed" search, or you can call 1-877-427-4088. A licence is not an endorsement of quality. Read the [Service Alberta prepaid contracting licence page](https://www.alberta.ca/prepaid-contracting-licence) before you sign anything.

Timing matters too. Sahi Contracting says most builds finish 3 to 5 weeks after permits. Hammerhead Construction says summer is the busy season and winter builds are possible, but concrete needs winter heat, which can add cost. In practice, start the permit conversation in winter and have two or three written quotes by early spring.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a garage permit in Calgary?

Yes, if the detached garage is greater than 10 m2 (107 sq ft), the City requires a building permit. It also needs separate trade permits, such as electrical. A development permit is added only if the garage triggers a bylaw rule, such as height over 4.6 m or a relaxation. Confirm the details for your lot with the City.

### How much does a Calgary garage permit cost?

For 2026, the City lists a new garage building permit at $321 plus a 4% Safety Codes Council fee, which is $333.84. A homeowner electrical permit is $116.50, and a development permit for a relaxation is $550. Fees can change, so check the City's current schedule.

### Who applies for the permit if I hire a builder?

The City says that if you hire someone to do the work, they will need to apply for the permit. Ask the builder to confirm in writing who applies, who pulls the electrical permit, and whether the fees are inside the quote. Only a Master Electrician can pull an electrical permit for a contractor.

### Can I use my Real Property Report as the site plan?

Yes, the City says you can use a Real Property Report as your site plan. Drawings marked "preliminary" or "for permit purposes only" are not accepted.
