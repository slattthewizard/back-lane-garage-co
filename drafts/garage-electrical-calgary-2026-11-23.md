# Garage Subpanel and Wiring in Calgary: Amperage, Upgrade Costs and Permits

Garage electrical is one of the quietest differences between two quotes. One package lists a 15 amp service and another lists a 40 amp subpanel, and both sit under the same heading of basic electrical. Years later, when someone wants a welder, a heater or an electric vehicle charger, the choice made at quote time decides how easy that is.

This page sets out what Calgary firms advertise for garage electrical, what upgrades and chargers cost according to their published pages, and what the City of Calgary says about electrical permits. A subpanel is a second breaker panel fed from the house. A 15 amp service and a 40 amp subpanel are different building blocks, so a quote that says only "electrical included" tells you very little. Ask for the amperage, whether it is 240V, whether there is a subpanel and how many circuits, outlets and lights the price covers.

BVM's cost guide is a cost guide with a different structure from a package price list, and BVM's pages disagree with one another in places, so read its figure as what BVM advertises rather than a Calgary benchmark. ## Upgrade prices firms publish

If a package starts at a lower amperage, the upgrade price matters.

- Calgary Garage Builders Ltd lists a 60 amp upgrade at $2,000 plus GST on its pricing page. A separate cost page on the same site gives approximately $1,500. When two pages disagree, get the number in writing.
- Polskiy Building publishes an Okotoks page that lists 240V subpanel upgrades at $900 for 40A, $1,000 for 50A and $1,200 for 60A, each plus GST. That list is labelled Okotoks. It is not a Calgary price, and you should not read it as one. If you are building in the town, see the [Okotoks garage builders page](/garage-builders-okotoks/).
- Falcon Contracting says basic electrical service runs $800 to $1,500.
- Precision Construction & Decora (PCND) says a 240V outlet for tools or an EV charger, a subpanel or extra lighting costs $2,000 to $6,000.

The ranges overlap in awkward ways. Falcon's $800 to $1,500 and PCND's $2,000 to $6,000 describe different scopes, and neither tells you what your garage needs. The way to compare is to ask each builder to price the same list of loads.

## EV chargers

Two firms give figures for chargers.

- Falcon Contracting says that if you are future-proofing, you should budget an additional $1,200 to $2,500 for an EV charging station.
- PCND includes a 240V outlet for an EV charger in its $2,000 to $6,000 range for a subpanel and extra electrical.

If you think a charger is likely, say so in your quote request, even if you will not buy one yet. A builder can then tell you what service they would run and what the conduit and panel space would cost. This site did not find a City rule that is specific to EV chargers, so ask the City or your electrical contractor about what applies to your install.

## The electrical permit is always required

The City of Calgary says that all new garages require a separate electrical permit for installing lights, outlets and the garage door. Its permitting page puts it this way: if you are building a new detached garage, an electrical permit is always needed to install lights and to connect the overhead door.

That holds even if the garage has only a light and an opener. Calgary builders who advertise the permit as included, such as Corsac with its garage electrical permit and inspection, are covering that cost in the package. Others may not, so check. If your garage will have a gas heater, a gas permit is a separate item. The [Calgary garage permit guide](/blog/calgary-garage-permit/) covers the full set of permits.

City pages say they have no legal status and cannot be used as an official interpretation of the bylaws, codes and regulations, so confirm anything here with the City.

## Who can pull the permit

There are two routes.

### A contractor

The City says that when an electrical contractor is doing the work, only a Master Electrician can obtain an electrical permit. If a garage builder is arranging the wiring, ask who the electrical contractor is, and ask to see that the permit is in that contractor's name. Alberta lists electrician as a compulsory certification trade, which means journeyperson certification or registration as an apprentice is compulsory for restricted activities in that trade.

A contractor trade permit costs $112 plus $9.79 per $1,000 of construction value, with a 4 per cent Safety Codes Council fee. The minimum total is $116.50.

### The homeowner

A homeowner can apply for a homeowner electrical permit in some cases. The City says the person must legally own the home and live in it, or intend to, so a rental does not qualify, and must do the work themselves. The City is clear that you cannot pull a permit on behalf of the individual or company doing the work.

A homeowner can add a subpanel to an existing main service, provided there is an existing main breaker. A homeowner cannot install, alter or modify the main electrical service, including the main panel, main breaker or the meter base. If your garage needs a bigger feed from the house, check whether the work touches the main service, because that part falls outside what a homeowner permit allows.

The 2026 homeowner electrical permit fee is $112 plus a $4.50 Safety Codes Council fee, for $116.50 in total.

## Inspection and concealed wiring

At inspection, the safety codes officer must be able to get to all areas where electrical work has been completed, and there must be no exposed live wiring. Wiring that has been covered before inspection is a problem. The City says a homeowner's permit cannot be issued for concealed wiring completed without proper permits, that the owner must hire a licensed electrical contractor, and that the inspector may ask for drywall to be removed.

Two practical steps follow. Do not let anyone close in the walls before the electrical inspection has been booked. And if work has already been done without a permit, speak to the City before doing anything else.

The City also says that if an applicant begins work before receiving a permit, the applicant is charged double the permit fee.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a detached garage need a subpanel in Calgary?

This site did not find a rule that says every garage must have one. Package inclusions vary from a 15 amp service to a 40 amp or 100 amp subpanel. What you need depends on the loads you plan to run, so ask your builder or electrical contractor.

### Do I need an electrical permit for a new garage in Calgary?

Yes. The City says an electrical permit is always needed for a new detached garage to install lights and connect the overhead door. The permit is separate from the building permit.

### Can I wire my own garage in Calgary?

A homeowner who owns and lives in the home can apply for a homeowner electrical permit and do the work themselves. The City allows adding a subpanel to an existing main service with a main breaker, but not changing the main panel, main breaker or meter base. Confirm the rules with the City first.

### Who can pull the electrical permit when a contractor does the work?

Only a Master Electrician. The City says that when an electrical contractor is doing the work, only a Master Electrician can obtain the electrical permit.
