# Cancel a Contractor Contract in Alberta: Garage Contract Rights and Checklist

Signing a garage contract at your kitchen table can feel like a small step. The sum is large and the work is outdoors, so it is easy to sign first and read second. Alberta's Consumer Protection Act gives homeowners a way out of some of these contracts, and it also sets out what a contract must say.

## When the cancellation rules apply

The rules in this article apply to a direct sales contract. The Act covers contracts "negotiated or concluded in person at a place other than the supplier's place of business". In plain terms, that means a builder came to you, or met you somewhere that is not the builder's own premises, and the contract was agreed or signed there.

The Government of Alberta's Consumer Bill of Rights puts it this way: "When a written contract has been solicited, agreed upon or signed at your home or at another location away from the contractor's normal place of business, the Consumer Protection Act allows you to cancel the contract up to 10 days after you receive your copy."

Here is where it stops. A contract you sign at the builder's office is not a direct sales contract under that definition, so the 10-day right described below does not apply. The same goes for a contract that is not covered by the Act's definitions for another reason. If you are not sure which side of the line your contract sits on, call the Consumer Contact Centre at 780-427-4088 in Edmonton and area, or 1-877-427-4088 toll free elsewhere in Alberta.

## The 10-day cancellation right

Section 27 of the Consumer Protection Act says a consumer "may, without any reason, cancel a direct sales contract at any time from the date the sales contract is entered into until, subject to the regulations, 10 days after the consumer receives a copy of the written sales contract."

Three points follow from that wording:

- You do not need a reason.
- The 10 days run from when you receive your copy of the written contract, not from when you sign.
- The Act says "subject to the regulations", so confirm the details with Service Alberta.

The Consumer Bill of Rights adds a rule for door-to-door sales: if the last day you can cancel falls on a Sunday or statutory holiday, you have one additional day. That passage sits in the door-to-door conditions, so ask Service Alberta whether it applies to your contract.

## Cancelling within one year

The 10-day window is not the only one. Section 28 of the Act allows cancellation within one year in some situations. Two of them are relevant to a garage:

1. The supplier was required to be licensed under the Act and was not licensed, or the contract does not include all the information required under section 35.
2. The supplier does not begin the services within 30 days from the commencement date specified in the direct sales contract.

The first one links to licensing. A builder who sells at your home and takes money before the work is done may need a prepaid contracting licence, which you can look up on Service Alberta's licence search. Check the licence before you pay.

The second one links to the start date. If your contract says work starts on a given day and nothing has happened 30 days later, you may have a right to cancel. Keep a record of the date the contract names and of what actually happened.

## How to give notice

Section 29(3) says notice may be given "by any means, including, but not limited to, personal service, registered mail, courier or telecopier or by any other method, including orally, by which the consumer can provide evidence of the date".

The point is evidence of the date. An oral notice is allowed in principle, but you would struggle to prove it. A sensible approach, and not a legal requirement, is to put it in writing and use a method that leaves a record:

- Send a short written notice that names the contract, the date and the address of the job.
- Use registered mail or a courier so you have a receipt, or deliver it in person and keep a copy.
- Keep the receipt, the tracking record and a photo of the letter.

The Act is about being able to show when you gave notice, so choose a method that does that.

## The 15-day refund

Section 31(2) says: "Within 15 days after a direct sales contract is cancelled, the supplier must refund to the consumer all money paid by the consumer".

So the refund is set by the Act at 15 days from cancellation. If a builder has taken a deposit and then does not refund it in that time, contact the Consumer Contact Centre. Keep your payment records. You may find them useful when you explain what happened.

## What a contract has to contain

Service Alberta publishes a contract requirements checklist, dated January 2019. It lists items that a contract should include. Use it as a checklist before you sign:

- The buyer's and the seller's names and addresses.
- A statement of cancellation rights.
- The itemized prices.
- The total amount.
- The terms of payment.
- The commencement and completion dates.
- Signatures.

Read the contract against that list. If a builder's contract leaves out your cancellation rights or the commencement date, ask for it to be corrected before you sign, because one of the one-year rights above depends on what the contract includes.

For a garage, also look for items that the checklist does not mention but that affect your price. These include the size, the slab, the wall height, the door, the electrical, who applies for the permits and who pays for them. ## A short list before you sign

1. Where was the contract negotiated and signed? Write it down.
2. Did you get a copy of the signed contract? Note the date you got it.
3. Does the contract state your cancellation rights?
4. Are the commencement and completion dates written in?
5. Is the builder's licence number on the paperwork, and does it show up in the Service Alberta search?
6. How much is due at signing, and what is due at each stage?
7. Is the permit plan written down?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I cancel a garage contract in Alberta without giving a reason?

For a direct sales contract, yes. The Consumer Protection Act lets a consumer cancel without any reason until 10 days after receiving a copy of the written contract. It applies to contracts negotiated or concluded in person away from the supplier's place of business, so a contract signed at the builder's office is outside it.

### How long does a builder have to refund my money after I cancel?

The Act says the supplier must refund all money paid within 15 days after a direct sales contract is cancelled. Keep your receipts and your proof of the date you gave notice. If the refund does not arrive, call the Consumer Contact Centre.

### Does a verbal cancellation count?

The Act allows notice by any method, including orally, that lets the consumer provide evidence of the date. Because proof is the difficulty, a written notice sent by registered mail or courier, or delivered with a copy kept, is easier to rely on.

### Is this legal advice?

No. This page summarizes what the Government of Alberta and the Consumer Protection Act say, and it can be out of date or miss details that apply to your contract. For advice on your own situation, contact Service Alberta or a lawyer.
