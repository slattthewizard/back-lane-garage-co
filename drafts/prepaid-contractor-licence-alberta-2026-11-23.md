# Prepaid Contractor Licence in Alberta: What to Check Before You Hire a Garage Builder

A garage contract often involves a builder visiting your property to measure and quote, and a payment before the work is finished. Alberta has a licence aimed at that combination: selling at your home and taking money up front.

This page explains what the prepaid contracting licence is, when a garage builder needs one, how to look a licence up, and what it does and does not tell you. ## What a prepaid contractor licence is

Service Alberta licenses prepaid contracting businesses. The Government of Alberta describes a prepaid contracting business as one that "solicits, negotiates or concludes prepaid contracts in person at any place other than the sellers' place of business" and "accepts money before all the work is done or the services are provided or both".

Two things have to be true together for the licence to apply:

- The contract is sold in person somewhere other than the seller's own place of business. Your kitchen table or your back yard counts.
- The seller takes money before all the work is finished.

The province defines prepaid contracts as contracts for "construction, maintenance, repairing, altering, adding to or improving private dwellings, or real property used in conjunction with a private dwelling such as landscaping services." A detached garage built at a house is real property used in conjunction with a private dwelling, so a garage contract that meets both tests falls within that description. Whether a specific builder needs the licence for a specific contract is a question for Service Alberta, not for this site.

To get the licence, a business has to supply business information, a criminal record check or police information check, a security, copies of its standard contracts and a licensing fee. The dollar amount of the security is not something this site could find published, so it is not quoted here.

## When it applies to a garage contract

A garage contract can meet both tests if the builder negotiates or signs it at your property and asks for money before the work is done. A builder who quotes by email and has you sign at the company office is in a different position, because the in-person condition is about where the contract is negotiated or concluded.

Alberta does not publish a deposit cap that this site could find, so there is no provincial rule you can point to that says a deposit must be below a set share of the price. That gap is a reason to read the payment terms in your contract line by line, and to compare them across builders. The [garage packages explained](/blog/garage-packages-explained/) page shows what a package usually covers, which helps when you compare payment terms against inclusions.

The City of Calgary also mentions the licence on its contractor page. A business that "will accept deposits or progress payments from homeowners before all work is complete" is told "You may need a Provincial Licence". Treat that as a prompt to ask, not as a ruling.

## How to check a licence on Service Alberta's search

Service Alberta runs a "Find out if a business is licensed" search on its website. The licence type to look for is "Prepaid Contractor". Steps:

1. Ask the builder for the exact legal name on the contract and the licence number, in writing.
2. Open the [Service Alberta licence search](https://www.servicealberta.ca/find-if-business-is-licenced.cfm) and enter the business name.
3. Check that the name, address and licence type match the paperwork you were handed.
4. Screenshot or print the result and keep it with your contract.

Note the limit of the search. It only lists active licences and registrations in Alberta, so a business that does not appear may be unlicensed, inactive or listed under a different legal name. Ask the builder which it is before you pay anything.

Licensed prepaid contractors are expected to provide confirmation of licensing on request. The province says that can include a link to the Find a Licensed Business search tool, so a builder who is licensed should have no trouble pointing you to it.

## Salesperson ID cards

The province also says that people selling for a prepaid contracting business must carry an ID card. The card shows the person's name, the business name, address and licence number. If someone arrives at your door to sell you a garage, ask to see the card and compare the licence number with the search result.

A builder who is quoting from an office, a website or a phone call may not be in a position where the card applies. If a salesperson is visiting you, though, asking for it is routine.

## A licence is not an endorsement

Service Alberta is plain about this. Its search page says licensing and registration "don t represent an endorsement of the company or charitable organization, nor do they guarantee the quality of goods or services offered."

So a licence tells you one thing: that the business holds a licence that was current on the day you looked. It does not tell you about the quality of the slab, the framing or the electrical. For those you rely on the written contract, the permits and the inspections.

If you have questions about a licence, Service Alberta's Consumer Contact Centre is 780-427-4088 in Edmonton and area, or 1-877-427-4088 toll free elsewhere in Alberta. The contract rules that sit alongside the licence include cancellation rights for sales made at your home.

## Other licences and certificates you may run into

The prepaid licence is one of several things that get lumped together as "the builder's licence". They are separate.

### City of Calgary business licence

The City of Calgary lists contractor businesses that "do require a licence". Its list includes "Builders", "General contractors" and "Specialty trade contractors, such as carpenters, electricians, gasfitters, plumbers, framers and welders". A City business licence is separate from the provincial prepaid contracting licence. Ask a builder which licences it holds, and check with the City if you are unsure, because City pages carry a disclaimer that they have no legal status.

### Compulsory trades

Alberta's list of compulsory certification trades includes electrician and gasfitter (Class A and Class B). For those trades, journeyperson certification or registration as an apprentice is compulsory to perform restricted activities. Carpenter does not appear on that list, as far as this site could tell from reading it.

That matters for a garage because the wiring is regulated differently from the framing. The City says that when an electrical contractor is doing the work, only a Master Electrician can obtain the electrical permit. If a builder subcontracts the wiring, ask who the electrical contractor is and who will pull the permit.

### Residential builder licence

Alberta requires a licence for residential builders: "All residential builders in Alberta must have a licence to obtain permits for constructing new homes." This site did not find any Alberta rule that requires a builder licence for a detached garage specifically. If a builder says one is required, or says one is not, ask them to show you the rule.

## New home warranty and detached garages

Homeowners sometimes assume a builder's warranty paperwork covers a detached garage the way it covers a new house. The provincial rules do not promise that. The Construction Performance Guide for New Home Warranty in Alberta says detached garages may be excluded from coverage, and the Home Warranty Insurance Regulation lets a warranty provider exclude certain construction.

A new detached garage built on its own for an existing home is not a new home. Do not assume any provincial warranty applies to it. Ask the builder in writing what they warrant, for how long, and what you do if there is a problem.

## What to put in front of a builder

Before you pay a deposit, ask for these in writing:

- The legal business name, address and licence number.
- A statement of which licences the builder holds and which trades are subcontracted.
- The permit plan: who applies, and who pays.
- Payment terms, including how much is due at signing and what is due at each stage.
- A statement of your cancellation rights.
- What the builder warrants after the work is done.

The City says that if you are hiring someone to do the work, they will need to apply for the permit. The [Calgary garage permit guide](/blog/calgary-garage-permit/) covers what the City requires. If a builder suggests starting before the permit is issued, note that the City says an applicant who begins work before receiving a permit is charged double the permit fee.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does every garage builder in Alberta need a prepaid contractor licence?

Not necessarily. The licence applies to a business that sells in person away from its own place of business and takes money before all the work is done. A builder whose contracts are negotiated and signed at its own office may not fall under it. Service Alberta can tell you how it applies to a particular contract.

### How do I check if a garage builder is licensed in Alberta?

Use the Service Alberta search called Find out if a business is licensed and look for the licence type Prepaid Contractor. It lists active licences only, so ask the builder for the exact legal name and licence number before you search.

### Does a licence mean the builder does good work?

No. Service Alberta says licensing does not represent an endorsement of a business and does not guarantee the quality of goods or services. Use the written contract, the permits and the inspections to protect the work itself.

### Is there a separate builder licence for building a garage in Alberta?

This site did not find an Alberta rule that requires a residential builder licence for a detached garage. The province's builder licence is described as being needed to obtain permits for constructing new homes. Ask any builder who says otherwise to point you to the rule.
