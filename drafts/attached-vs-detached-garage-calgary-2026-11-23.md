# Attached vs Detached Garage in Calgary: Rules, Costs and Trade-offs

Choosing between an attached and a detached garage looks like a layout decision. In Calgary it is also a classification decision, because the City treats the two as different kinds of project with different rules. One is a garage. The other is an addition to your house.

## How the City classifies each one

The City's garage page is direct about this. A garage attached to the house "is considered an addition and not a garage or similar structure." A detached garage, along with sheds, pergolas and similar buildings, is an accessory residential building under the Land Use Bylaw.

That classification changes the application. For a detached garage the City says you need a Residential Improvement Project Permit, and a building permit is required if the garage is larger than 10 square metres (107 square feet). An attached garage is reviewed as part of a house addition, under the rules for the house. The sources this site relies on do not set out those addition rules, so if you are considering an attached garage, ask the City which permits and approvals apply.

## Permit fees

For a new detached garage, the City's 2026 building permit fee is $321, made up of $112 processing and $209 base, plus a 4 per cent Safety Codes Council fee of $12.84. The total is $333.84. A separate electrical permit is always required for the lights and the overhead door connection. If the garage meets every Land Use Bylaw rule, no development permit is needed.

The City's fee schedule groups its lines by project type. The line that carries the $333.84 figure reads "Basement, garage, addition under 400 square feet". A separate line, "Addition over 400 square feet", is $1,302.08. The City's garage page lists a single new-garage fee with no size split. Which line applies to an attached garage depends on how the City classifies the project and its size, so ask the City before you assume either figure. Do not read the $1,302.08 as a detached garage fee. It is not.

## Lane access and the driveway

This is where the two types often part ways on a Calgary lot.

A detached garage at the back of the lot is usually reached from the lane. The City requires the driveway that connects a lane to a garage to be at least 0.6 metres long, measured from the property line shared with the lane to the garage door. The City calls that the apron.

A garage reached from the street has bigger requirements. A driveway from a street to a private garage must be a minimum of 6.0 metres long and contain a rectangular area measuring 6.0 metres by 3.0 metres. In the Developed Area, the City says a driveway accessing a street must not be constructed, altered or replaced unless one of these applies: the parcel is laneless, 50 per cent or more of parcels on the block face already have a street driveway, or a legally existing driveway is not being relocated or widened.

So a front-attached garage on a lot with a lane may not be possible in the Developed Area if the block does not already have street driveways. The sources do not describe the City's rule for an attached garage reached from a lane, so confirm that one directly.

## Distance from the house and fire protection

A detached garage has a rule an attached one does not need: a gap. The minimum distance between any façade of an accessory building of 10 square metres or more and the house is 1.0 metre. The City says this includes second floor cantilevers and bay windows. Closer than 1.0 metre needs a development permit. That is a fee of $550 and a stated timeline of 10 to 12 weeks, against about 7 days for a building permit, and the City says timelines are not guaranteed.

Setbacks from lot lines also apply to a detached garage: 0.6 metres from a side or rear line in most cases, or 1.2 metres from a line shared with a street. Near the side line, the City says 5/8 inch Type X drywall is required from floor to ceiling on all interior walls within 0.6 metres of the side property line, and the framing inspection checks that fire-rated materials are installed. Eaves have limits too: the City applies a building-code rule that eave projections are not permitted within 0.45 metres of a side property line shared with an adjacent property.

The sources this site read do not describe the equivalent rules for an attached garage, which sits against the house and is governed by the house's rules. Ask the City.

## Parking stalls count either way

The City says at least one parking stall is required in most land use districts. Stalls cannot be in tandem. A stall that touches two barriers, such as the walls of a garage, is at least 5.9 m x 3 m, and the City says garage walls count as barriers. That holds whether the garage is attached or detached, so check that the stall fits inside the finished walls, not the outside dimensions. Not all stalls need to be inside a garage.

## Height, size and use of a detached garage

A detached garage must not exceed 4.6 metres in height, or 3.0 metres at any eaveline, measured from the finished floor, and it is limited to one storey. All accessory buildings together must not exceed the lesser of the house's building coverage or 75 square metres. A garage larger than 75 square metres, or larger than the house, needs a development permit.

The City classes an attached garage as an addition, so ask which height and size limits apply to it. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is an attached garage treated differently by the City of Calgary?

Yes. The City says a garage attached to the house is considered an addition and not a garage or similar structure. A detached garage is an accessory residential building under the Land Use Bylaw.

### Which costs less per square foot, attached or detached?

Falcon Contracting states $40 to $70 per square foot for detached and $35 to $50 for attached. That is one firm's figure, and no other Calgary builder in the sources states a per-square-foot price for both.

### How far must a detached garage be from the house?

At least 1.0 metre between any façade of the garage and the house, according to the City. Closer than that needs a development permit.

### Can I put a driveway to a garage from the street in Calgary?

In the Developed Area, a street driveway cannot be built, altered or replaced unless the parcel is laneless, 50 per cent or more of the block face already has street driveways, or an existing driveway is not being relocated or widened. Confirm your lot with the City.
