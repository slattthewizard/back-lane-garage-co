# Garage Demolition in Calgary: Permit Rules, Removal Prices and What Comes Next

An old detached garage with a sagging roof and a heaved slab is easy to want gone. What is less obvious is whether the City wants paperwork before it comes down, what the removal costs, and what happens to your property assessment once the building is no longer there.

This page covers the rules the City publishes for taking down a detached garage, the fee basis for a demolition permit, and the removal prices two Calgary-area firms advertise. If you would rather talk it through, [call (403) 555-0162](tel:+14035550162). Independent Calgary garage builders quote and contract with you directly, and any permit is applied for by whoever does the work.

City pages on this subject say they have no legal status and cannot be used as an official interpretation of the bylaws. Treat everything below as a guide and confirm your own situation with the City before work starts.

## Do you need a demolition permit to remove a detached garage?

In most cases, no. The City's demolition page says a demolition permit is not required for removing a detached residential garage. The exceptions are narrow, and they turn on utilities rather than on the size of the building.

For a detached garage over 10 m² (107 sq ft), a demolition permit is only required if one of these is true:

- The garage or accessory building is independently serviced.
- It holds the primary utility meters or connections for the house.

No demolition permit is needed if the garage has no utility services at all. No demolition permit is needed either if the garage will be demolished and replaced with another garage or accessory building, because the City says the demolition of the existing structure is then included in the permit for the new structure.

Independently serviced is the phrase to watch. A garage with its own electrical service, or a separate gas line feeding a heater, may fall on the permit side of the line. If you are not sure how the City would class yours, ask before you book a crew.

## When a permit does apply: the asbestos form

Where a demolition permit is required, the City says the applicant must submit the asbestos abatement form before starting demolition. That step comes before the work, not after.

The City's page does not tell you how to decide whether an old garage contains asbestos, and this page will not guess. If your garage is old enough that you wonder about it, raise it with the person quoting the removal and ask how they handle the City's form. Ask them to put that answer in writing too.

## What the demolition permit fee is based on

The City's 2026 Building and Trade Permit Fee Schedule lists the demolition permit fee as $112 plus $1.44 per square metre, with the 4% Safety Codes Council levy on top.

Here is plain arithmetic on that figure, assuming the area is the garage footprint. The City decides what area counts, so this is an illustration of scale and not a quote.

| Garage size | Area | Fee before levy | With 4% levy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 x 20 ft | about 37.2 m² | about $166 | about $172 |
| 24 x 24 ft | about 53.5 m² | about $189 | about $197 |

The permit fee is a small part of what removal costs. The bigger numbers are labour, disposal and the slab.

One more fee rule matters here. The City's fee schedule says that if an applicant begins work before receiving a permit, the applicant is charged double the permit fee.

## What a builder charges to remove a garage

Corsac Construction advertises a standard price to remove an existing detached garage starting at $17.50 per square foot. That is what that firm advertises on its own page. It is not a Calgary-wide price, and the line does not state whether GST is added. Corsac's package prices are shown plus GST, so ask how it treats this line on your quote.

Corsac says its demolition includes:

- removal and disposal of existing materials, including the concrete pad
- fencing removal
- wall and roof structure removal
- electrical and gas line removal and capping

Using the same arithmetic as above, the advertised rate works out to about $7,000 for a 20x20 garage (400 sq ft) and about $10,080 for a 24x24 (576 sq ft). Those are multiplications of one firm's starting rate by floor area, not a quote. Your lot, your access and what is under the building will move the real figure.

Because a garage removal line mixes the pad, the walls, and the utility work, two quotes can look far apart without being comparable. Ask each firm to list what is in the price: the slab, the footings, disposal fees, fence panels, and what happens to the gas and electrical lines once the building is gone.

## Breaking up the concrete

The slab is often the part people do not price separately. Alta Home publishes a price list covering Calgary and Edmonton together, and it shows concrete breaking and haul away at $6 per square foot. That list is not specific to Calgary, so read it as one firm's published rate and not as a Calgary figure.

At that rate, the same arithmetic gives about $2,400 for a 400 sq ft slab and about $3,456 for 576 sq ft. Corsac lists the concrete pad inside its removal line, so if you are comparing the two, check whether one figure already includes what the other lists separately.

Whether the old slab can be kept is a different question. AB Garage Builders says an existing parking pad needs assessment before garage construction. A builder looking at your site can tell you whether the old slab is usable or has to come out first.

## Demolition and the new garage under one permit

If you are tearing the old garage down to build a new one, the City's rule helps. No separate demolition permit is needed when the existing garage is replaced with another garage or accessory building, because the demolition is included in the permit for the new structure.

That also means the sequence matters. The building permit for the new garage is the one that carries the demolition, so the application should describe both. The City lists the building permit fee for a new garage at $333.84 for 2026, and a building permit review at approximately 7 days. Both depend on a complete application, and the City says timelines are not a guarantee. If the new garage needs a development permit, that is a much longer process, with a City timeline of 10 to 12 weeks.

For the permit side generally, see [the Calgary garage permit guide](/blog/calgary-garage-permit/). If the plan is a new building on the same spot, the [garage replacement page](/garage-replacement/) covers how replacement jobs are usually quoted.

## What demolition does to your property assessment

The City says that if you demolish a detached garage or accessory buildings over 10 m² in addition to the main building, you must include it on the site plan and in the demolition area calculations, and that property assessment and property taxes will be affected. That line is aimed at demolishing a house with its outbuildings.

For the more common case, tearing down a garage and not replacing it, the City says to contact property assessment. The City's assessment page also lists the existence, type and size of a garage among the factors that may affect market value. This page cannot tell you what a garage adds to or removes from your assessment, because the City publishes no such figure. If you are not building a replacement, make that call before the garage comes down.

## Before the crew arrives

A few practical points that come straight from the City's pages:

- Utilities. Corsac lists electrical and gas line removal and capping inside its removal price. Ask any firm quoting you who does that work and who holds any permit it needs.
- Digging. The City tells homeowners to contact Click Before You Dig before any excavation, which applies if footings or buried services are coming out.
- Easements. Permanent structures are not permitted in utility rights-of-way, so check your title for a registered one before you plan what replaces the garage.
- Records. If you will be building again, a Real Property Report can serve as the site plan for the new application.

## Getting the removal priced

Removal prices vary with the footprint, the slab, access from the lane and what is connected to the building. Ask for the same scope from each firm so the figures line up: demolition, concrete, disposal, utility capping and whether the new garage is going in at the same time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a permit to tear down a detached garage in Calgary?

Usually not. The City says a demolition permit is not required for removing a detached residential garage in most cases. One is required for a garage over 10 m² only if it is independently serviced or holds the house's primary utility connections, so confirm with the City.

### What does a demolition permit cost in Calgary?

The City's 2026 fee schedule lists the demolition permit fee as $112 plus $1.44 per square metre, with a 4% Safety Codes Council levy on top. On a 20x20 garage that arithmetic comes to about $172. The City decides what area counts and whether the fee applies to your garage.

### Does the demolition need a separate permit if I am building a new garage?

No. The City says that when the old garage will be demolished and replaced with another garage or accessory building, the demolition is included in the permit for the new structure. Starting work before a needed permit is issued costs double the permit fee.

### Will taking the garage down change my property assessment?

It may. The City lists the existence, type and size of a garage among the factors that may affect market value, and says to contact property assessment if you are demolishing a garage without replacing it. The City publishes no figure for what a garage adds to an assessment.
