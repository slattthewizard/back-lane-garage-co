# Building a Garage in Winter in Calgary: What Builders Say

Calgary winters make people assume a garage build has to wait for May. Two Calgary builders say otherwise, with conditions. Winter work is possible, it can cost more, and the better use of the cold months for most homeowners is getting permits and quotes done so the build can start as soon as the ground allows.

This page sets out what the builders we read actually say about winter, what the City says about timing, and how to plan a build that starts in spring. If you want to talk it over, [call (403) 555-0162](tel:+14035550162). Independent Calgary garage builders quote and contract with you directly.

## Can you build a garage in winter?

Yes, according to the two builders that address it directly.

- Falcon Contracting: winter builds are possible with specialized concrete techniques, though they may incur slight additional heating costs.
- Hammerhead Construction: garages can be built in the cooler months, but will require winter heat in order to pour the concrete, and this can add to the cost.

Neither page gives a dollar figure for the winter premium, so there is no number to quote here. The cost of heating the pour is a line to ask each builder to price separately. Falcon also says that standard garage builds typically take 4 to 8 weeks from excavation to inspection, though permits and weather can affect timelines. Sahi Contracting says most construction is completed within 3 to 5 weeks once permits are in hand, depending on Calgary's seasonal weather.

We did not find a City of Calgary statement on winter garage construction. The City publishes permit requirements and inspection rules, and none of what we read is season-specific. Nothing here says winter building is banned, and nothing says it is simple.

## Why concrete is the hard part

The slab is the step most affected by cold, which is why both builders mention concrete. Hammerhead ties winter work to heat for the pour. Falcon refers to specialized concrete techniques. Neither goes into detail on the page, so ask each builder to describe how they would protect and cure a slab poured in January, and what they would charge for it.

Calgary soil is another builder-side concern. Precision Construction & Decora says Calgary's clay soil and freeze-thaw cycles mean the foundation has to be built to handle ground movement. That is a builder statement. BVM Builders specifies perimeter footings at a minimum 1.2 m below grade, which it describes as Calgary frost depth, on its own builds. That is BVM's specification, not a City requirement for a garage under 55 m2.

What the City does say is that a garage slab over 55 m2 (592 sq ft) without a concrete foundation 1.2 m below grade needs a slab designed by a professional engineer. If you are weighing slab size, the [double garage size guide](/blog/double-garage-size/) covers how 20x20, 22x22 and 24x24 sit against that line.

## Summer is the busy season

Hammerhead says more projects are completed in the summer months, which means demand for product and labour can be higher during busy periods. That is the builder's own account of its market.

We cannot tell you that winter is cheaper. None of the builders we read publishes a winter discount, and Hammerhead and Falcon both say winter work can add heating cost. What winter does give you is time. Quotes, drawings and permits do not need frozen ground, and doing them early means you are ready to book a start when the weather turns.

## Permit timing is not seasonal, and not guaranteed

The City of Calgary lists a building permit for a new garage at approximately 7 days, and a development permit, when one is needed, at 10 to 12 weeks (60 days to a decision plus a 21-day advertisement and appeal period). The City adds that these timelines are not a guarantee and depend on volumes, application completeness and how quickly the applicant answers questions.

The City's page also said it was currently experiencing higher than seasonal application volumes and apologized for delays. That is a note on the page we read, not a forecast for next spring. Plan on the basis that the stated times can slip.

City pages carry a note that the information has no legal status, so confirm timing and requirements with the City. The [permit guide](/blog/calgary-garage-permit/) explains when a garage needs a development permit as well as a building permit.

Two timing rules matter for a winter plan:

- A building permit expires 180 days from issuance. A permit issued on 1 December would run out around the end of May (arithmetic on the City's 180 days, not a date the City gives). If you are applying early to be ready for spring, check the dates with your builder so the permit does not lapse before work starts.
- The City may refuse an application that is incomplete if adequate information is not supplied within 90 days of receipt. Winter is a good time to assemble the site plan and drawings correctly the first time.

Fees are paid in full at application. The City's 2026 fee for a new-garage building permit is $333.84, and if work starts before a permit is issued the City charges double the permit fee.

## A plan that uses the winter months

If your goal is a garage ready for the busy season, the work that can happen in the cold months is mostly paperwork.

1. Decide size, door and whether the garage will be heated. Ask each builder to state the door size in the quote.
2. Collect written quotes from several independent builders, with the same size, walls, door and electrical in each.
3. Get a Real Property Report or site plan. The City says you can use a Real Property Report as your site plan.
4. Confirm with the City whether a development permit is needed, and allow 10 to 12 weeks if it is.
5. Agree on a start window with your builder, and ask what a winter start would cost against a spring one.

Here is the timing as plain arithmetic on the published figures. If no development permit is needed, the City's approximately 7 days for the building permit plus Sahi's 3 to 5 weeks of construction, or Falcon's 4 to 8 weeks from excavation to inspection, puts a finished garage roughly one to two months after a complete application. If a development permit is needed, add the City's 10 to 12 weeks first. None of these figures is a promise, and weather and volumes can move them. Work back from the date you want the garage finished, and you will see why early quotes matter.

Packages differ in what they include, including the permit work. Venkor says city permits and design drawings are included in its quote. Corsac says the garage electrical permit and inspection are included. Others may not do the same, so ask.

## Cold-weather costs to ask about

Because no builder gives a winter premium, the way to learn the number is to ask for it. Request a quote for the same garage in two scenarios, one for a winter pour and one for a spring pour, and ask the builder to show the heating line.

For the wider picture of what a garage costs and what is in a package, see [the cost to build a garage in Calgary](/blog/cost-to-build-a-garage-calgary/). Heating the finished garage is a separate cost again. Precision Construction & Decora says a forced-air or radiant tube heater adds $2,500 to $6,000, and Corsac advertises a gas heater package at $6,600 that includes the heater, underground line installation, trenching, permitting and dedicated electrical services. Those are what those firms advertise, not figures from this site.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you pour a garage slab in winter in Calgary?

Falcon Contracting says winter builds are possible with specialized concrete techniques, and Hammerhead Construction says concrete needs winter heat to pour. Both say it can add cost. Neither gives a figure, so ask each builder to price it.

### Does the City of Calgary say anything about building a garage in winter?

We did not find any City statement on winter garage construction. None of the City permit pages we read is season-specific. Its building permit is listed at approximately 7 days and a development permit at 10 to 12 weeks, with no guarantee.

### Is it cheaper to build a garage in winter?

We found no builder that advertises a winter discount. Hammerhead says more projects are completed in summer, and Hammerhead and Falcon both say winter work can add heating cost. Whether a winter slot saves or costs you depends on the quote.

### How long does a building permit last once it is issued?

The City lists building permit expiry at 180 days from issuance. If you obtain a permit over winter for a spring build, confirm the start date with your builder so the permit does not expire first.
