# Building a Garage on an Existing Parking Pad in Calgary

A parking pad feels like a head start. The concrete is already poured and it already sits at the back of the lot. Whether it can carry a garage is a separate question, and the answer depends on its size, its position and what is under it.

This page covers what a garage on an existing parking pad involves in Calgary: what the City says about pouring a pad with a garage in mind, the slab rules that decide whether a pad can carry a building, the setbacks and apron that apply, and the pad prices builders publish. ## What the City says about pouring a pad for a future garage

The City's garage page makes a point that applies to anyone thinking of pouring concrete now and building later. It says a building permit is not required for a parking pad. It then adds that if you plan to pour a parking pad for the purpose of building a garage in the future, you should consider the location against the setback and design rules.

That is the useful part. A pad has no permit, so nobody checks where you put it. A garage does get checked, and the garage has to fit the rules whether or not the pad was poured first. A pad placed 0.3 metres from the side line, or only 0.8 metres from the house, may be a fine parking pad and a poor garage footprint.

Rocky View County makes a related point from the other side. It lists parking pads and garages among projects that do not need a development permit when they comply with the Land Use Bylaw. Compliance is the condition in both places.

## Does the pad need assessing first?

AB Garage Builders, a Calgary firm, says that an existing parking pad needs assessment before garage construction. The page does not list what the assessment covers, so ask any builder you speak to what they check and what they would charge to check it.

The City publishes the rules that make the assessment matter. They are about slab size and garage construction type:

- Under City Regulation Bulletin RB14-011, which quotes the building code, detached garages of less than 55 square metres of floor area and no more than one storey are permitted to sit on wood mud sills or a 100 millimetre thick concrete floor slab. The garage cannot be of masonry or masonry veneer construction. The bulletin was last updated in 2015 and refers to the 2014 code.
- The City's garage page says a slab bigger than 55 square metres (592 square feet) that does not have a concrete foundation 1.2 metres below grade needs a concrete slab designed by a professional engineer.
- RB14-011 also allows a larger garage on a slab if it is structurally compartmentalized into 55 square metre sections, with nothing crossing the compartment line.

Two things follow. First, if your pad is under 55 square metres and you plan a garage of the same size, the slab-thickness rule is a specific number a builder can check. Second, if you want a bigger garage than the pad, or the pad is larger than 55 square metres, the engineering question changes.

Builders say the same thing in their own words. Precision Construction & Decora says a standard detached garage under 55 square metres can sit on a properly built floating slab on grade. Corsac Construction says slabs exceeding 55 square metres (592 square feet) need an engineered foundation design. The rule is not a builder's preference. It is a size threshold, and a pad poured for parking does not tell you which side of it you are on.

How thick the existing concrete is, what is under it and whether it has cracked are questions for the person who inspects it. This site does not make that call, and nothing here replaces an assessment.

## Where the garage can go on the lot

Placing the garage on the pad is a placement question first. The City's rules for an accessory building of 10 square metres or more set the limits:

- Side or rear line: at least 0.6 metres in most cases, and at least 1.2 metres from a side or rear line shared with a street.
- House: at least 1.0 metre between any façade of the garage and the house. Closer than that needs a development permit.
- Front: the garage must not be in the actual front setback area.
- Lane apron: a driveway connecting a lane to a garage must be at least 0.6 metres long, measured from the property line shared with the lane to the garage door.

The apron is where existing pads often fall short. If a pad ends at the lane property line, a garage door built right at its edge leaves no apron.

Total size also matters. All accessory buildings together must not exceed the lesser of the house's building coverage or 75 square metres, and a garage over 75 square metres needs a development permit. ## Parking stalls: the pad may be your required parking

The City says at least one parking stall is required in most land use districts. If the pad is currently your required stall, building over it can mean the garage has to supply the stall instead. The City's stall sizes depend on what the stall touches:

- Touching two barriers: at least 5.9 m x 3 m.
- Touching one barrier: 5.9 m x 2.85 m.
- No barriers: 5.9 m x 2.5 m.

Garage walls count as barriers. A single stall inside a garage, with a wall on each side, is therefore the larger 5.9 m x 3 m size, which is wider than the open stall on the pad had to be. Stalls cannot be in tandem, and the City notes that not all stalls need to be inside a garage.

Check that the inside of the finished garage still meets the stall size once wall thickness is taken out. Builders quote outside dimensions, and the inside is smaller.

## What pads cost, and what that tells you

Concrete is the one part of the project you may already own. These are the pad prices builders publish, each from the firm's own site and not a market average.

Patriarch Construction, in its 2026 guide, says most pads fall between $4,500 and $8,000, depending on size, thickness, base preparation and access. By size, it gives these ranges for a standard and a high-cost job:

| Pad size | Standard | High cost |
|---|---|---|
| Single car, about 240 sq ft | $2,000 to $3,000 | $3,500 to $4,500 |
| Small double, about 440 sq ft | $4,500 to $6,000 | $6,000 to $8,000 |

Falcon Contracting says concrete slab foundations typically cost $5.50 to $9.00 per square foot, which it works out to $2,200 to $3,600 for a standard 400 square foot foundation. GST treatment is not stated for either firm.

Those are pad-only figures. Garage packages from other firms include the slab, so the pad is not a separate line in the same way. Venkor Group lists a 4 inch concrete slab with a 6 inch curb wall in its packages, and Corsac lists a 4 to 5 inch pad with a 6 inch curb. If an existing pad can be used, a builder may be able to price a garage without a new slab. If it cannot be used, there is another cost to weigh.

Removal has its own price. Corsac says removing an existing detached garage starts at $17.50 per square foot, including the concrete pad. Alta Home lists concrete breaking and haul-away at $6 per square foot, a price that covers Calgary and Edmonton together. Compare those against the figures above when you decide between keeping and replacing a pad. ## Permits and who applies

The garage itself needs a building permit if it is larger than 10 square metres. The City's 2026 fee for a new garage is $321 plus a 4 per cent Safety Codes Council fee, a total of $333.84, and a separate electrical permit is always needed for the lights and overhead door connection. A garage that meets every Land Use Bylaw rule needs no development permit. If you hire a builder, they apply for the permits. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a permit to pour a parking pad in Calgary?

The City says a building permit is not required for a parking pad. It does advise considering the location if you plan to build a garage on it later, because the garage has to meet the setback and design rules.

### Can I build a garage directly on my existing parking pad?

Maybe. AB Garage Builders says an existing pad needs assessment before garage construction. The City's slab rules turn on size, with 55 square metres as the threshold for slab-on-grade construction without an engineered design.

### How far does the garage need to be from the lane?

The City requires a driveway apron of at least 0.6 metres between the lane property line and the garage door. The garage must also be at least 0.6 metres from a side or rear line in most cases, and 1.0 metre from the house.

### Does Rocky View County need a development permit for a garage on a pad?

Rocky View County says parking pads and garages do not need a development permit if they comply with its Land Use Bylaw. Building permits are required for buildings over 10 square metres, so check with the County before you start.
