# Garage Height in Calgary: Limits, Tall Doors and Area Town Rules

Height is the number people forget until the truck, the lift or the camper does not fit. In Calgary it is also one of the numbers that can push a garage from a quick building permit into a development permit. Two limits sit close together, and they are measured from different places.

This page sets out the City's height limits for a detached garage, the 3 m line that triggers a development permit, the attic rules, what Calgary builders advertise for wall height and doors, and the limits in Airdrie, Cochrane, Okotoks and Chestermere. ## The Land Use Bylaw height limit

The Land Use Bylaw says the height of an accessory residential building, which includes a detached garage, must not exceed:

- 4.6 m (15 ft 1 in), measured from the finished floor of the building
- 3.0 m (9 ft 10 in) at any eaveline, measured from the finished floor
- one storey

All three apply together. The 4.6 m limit caps the peak, the 3.0 m limit caps where the wall meets the roof edge, and the one-storey rule rules out a second floor. A garage with a roof pitch that keeps the eaveline at or under 3.0 m and the peak at or under 4.6 m meets the first two.

The City's pages show the conversion for 4.6 m both as 15 ft and as 15 ft 1 in. Work in metres, because the bylaw does.

## The 3 m line that triggers a development permit

A separate City rule uses a different measurement. A development permit is required if the height from the parking surface to where the wall meets the roof truss is higher than 3 m (9 ft 10 in). It is also required if the garage is higher than 4.6 m.

The two 3 m figures look alike but are not the same measurement. The bylaw's eaveline limit is measured from the finished floor. The permit trigger is measured from the parking surface to the wall-to-truss point. Your site plan has to show both: the City's checklist asks for the height from the parking surface to where the wall meets the roof and from the parking surface to the peak.

A development permit costs $550 in 2026 and the City lists 10 to 12 weeks for a decision, against about 7 days for a building permit. There is no guarantee of approval. The full list of triggers and fees is in [the Calgary garage permit guide](/blog/calgary-garage-permit/).

## Attics inside the single storey

The bylaw allows an attic within that one storey, with conditions. The attic must:

- be accessed by a removable ladder
- have no windows
- be used by the occupants of the main house for placing personal items
- have a maximum height of 1.5 m, measured from the attic floor to the underside of any rafter

A fixed staircase, a window or a taller loft does not fit that description. A garage with a loft you can stand in is a different design. Ask the City before you pay for drawings.

The bylaw also says an accessory building must not have a balcony or rooftop deck, so a plan with one is not a height question. The City says a proposed rooftop deck or balcony always needs a development permit.

Whether a 9.5 ft wall stays under the City's trigger depends on how the wall-to-truss height is measured from the parking surface on your slab, so ask the builder to state that number on the drawings.

## Tall doors and trusses

A wide, tall door carries more of the roof. The City's garage checklist asks for beam calculations or manufacturer truss layouts only if the roof trusses are resting on the garage door. A double door wide enough for a high-roof vehicle may be that case, which adds paper to the permit.

Installed door prices are published for standard sizes. Prime Garage Doors Calgary lists insulated steel doors in its 2026 guide at $1,499 to $2,099 for a single and $2,099 to $3,399 for a double, installed, with GST not stated. Those are standard-size figures from one door company and do not cover a tall or oversized opening.

Roof trusses have their own rule from 1 March 2026. Rocky View County says all truss profiles and layouts require an engineer's stamp unless complete design information is authenticated through other documentation, and Airdrie says all truss specifications must include an Alberta engineer's seal. No City of Calgary page stating the truss rule was found, so ask your builder how it handles trusses for your height.

Neither is a price for an RV garage. A tall RV door also runs into the limits above: with a 3.0 m eaveline cap and a 4.6 m overall cap, the opening you can fit is constrained by the bylaw before it is constrained by the builder. If a vehicle needs more than the bylaw allows, the route is a relaxation through a development permit, which is not guaranteed. ## Height limits in nearby towns

Calgary's rules do not carry over the city limits. Each town sets its own.

| Town | Height rule published |
|---|---|
| Airdrie | Ground to peak must not exceed 5 m (16 ft 5 in) or the height of the principal dwelling, whichever is smaller |
| Cochrane | Flat roof 4.0 m, peaked roof 4.6 m; no development permit at 53.5 m² or less and 4.6 m or less |
| Okotoks | Not permitted to exceed the height of the principal building |
| Chestermere | 4.5 m for accessory buildings in the R-1 district |

The Airdrie page was read from an archived copy dated 14 August 2026, because the Town site was unreachable. Cochrane's figures come from its Land Use Bylaw 01/2022 and Chestermere's from Land Use Bylaw 020-24. Okotoks says a development permit is required if the structure is taller than the primary building.

Rocky View County's accessory building height limits vary by district and were not confirmed for this page, so ask the County. Confirm any town figure with the town before you design.

## Choosing a height before you ask for quotes

Start with what has to fit and then check it against the numbers above.

1. Measure the tallest vehicle with roof racks, and the door opening it needs.
2. Decide whether you want a 9 ft ceiling, a taller door, or neither.
3. Ask each builder to state wall height, ceiling height, door size and the wall-to-truss height measured from the parking surface.
4. Ask whether the design stays under 4.6 m and the 3 m trigger, or needs a development permit.
5. Ask for a written price for any tall-door or ceiling upgrade, since none is published.

Every builder should quote the same wall height and door so the numbers line up. For more on how packages are put together, see [garage packages explained](/blog/garage-packages-explained/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the maximum garage height in Calgary?

The Land Use Bylaw limits a detached garage to 4.6 m measured from the finished floor, 3.0 m at any eaveline, and one storey. A garage higher than 4.6 m needs a development permit. Confirm your lot with the City, since City pages have no legal status.

### How tall can a garage door be in Calgary?

The sources behind this page give no door height limit. The wall and roof limits decide what fits. Corsac Construction lists 18x7 and 18x8 insulated doors and a 9 ft ceiling as options, with no price. Ask builders for written quotes on the size you need.

### Can I build an RV garage in Calgary?

It depends on the height of the vehicle against the limits above. No Calgary builder price for an RV garage was found. An oversized or tall-door garage may need a development permit, which is not guaranteed.

### Can a garage have an attic or loft?

An attic is allowed within the single storey if it is reached by a removable ladder, has no windows, is used for personal items, and is no more than 1.5 m from the attic floor to the underside of any rafter. A garage with a living loft is a different design.
