The setback rules decide where a detached garage can sit on a Calgary lot, and on a narrow lot they often decide how wide it can be. Most of them are short numbers: 0.6 m, 1.2 m, 1.0 m. A few have conditions attached that change the answer, and one small mistake on a site plan is enough for the City to refuse an application.

This page walks through the distances the City publishes for a detached garage, from the side and rear lines to the house, then the eave and fire-protection rules that apply when you build close to a neighbour. City pages on this subject say they have no legal status and cannot be used as an official interpretation of the bylaws. The numbers below come from the City's pages and the Land Use Bylaw text. Confirm your own lot with the City before you commit to a position.

## The basic setbacks: 0.6 m and 1.2 m

The Land Use Bylaw sets the minimum setback for an accessory residential building, which includes a detached garage, at:

- 1.2 m from a side or rear property line that is shared with a street
- 0.6 m from a side or rear property line in all other cases

For a garage on a typical interior lot with a lane at the back, that means 0.6 m from the neighbour's side line. A corner lot is the case where the 1.2 m figure shows up, on the line it shares with the street.

The bylaw wording says "street". The sources behind this page do not say how a lane is classed under that wording, so confirm with the City how your rear line against a lane is treated before you fix the garage position.

## Where a zero setback is allowed

The 0.6 m setback may be reduced to zero in three situations:

1. The building is less than 10 m² (107 sq ft) in floor area. That rules out a garage, but it matters for sheds beside it.
2. The garage wall is built of maintenance-free materials and there is no overhang of eaves onto the neighbouring parcel.
3. The owner of the adjacent parcel grants a 1.5 m private maintenance easement.

The City's page names vinyl siding and stucco as examples of maintenance-free materials. If you plan a different finish on a wall at the line, ask the builder and the City whether it qualifies.

The easement route is a legal document, not a handshake. The bylaw calls for a 1.5 m private maintenance easement registered on both titles, along with a 0.60 m eave and footing encroachment easement. That needs the neighbour's agreement. Without it, the maintenance-free wall with no eave overhang is the other way to zero.

Building on the line has a catch too. The wall is on the line, so the footing, the eave and the gutter all have to stay on your side of it. See the eave rules below.

## No garage in the front setback

An accessory residential building must not be located in the actual front setback area. A detached garage therefore goes behind the front setback, not out front.

A related rule applies to laneless parcels. A private garage on a parcel with no lane may be located within the required 3.0 m side setback area, except along the street side of a corner parcel.

## Distance from the house: 1.0 m

An accessory building of 10 m² or more must have at least 1.0 m between any façade and the main residential building. The City adds that this includes second-floor cantilevers and bay windows on the house, so measure from the furthest-out part of the house and not the foundation line.

Closer than 1.0 m is possible, but it needs a development permit with its own fee and a wait of about 10 to 12 weeks per the City's timeline. The [garage permit guide](/blog/calgary-garage-permit/) covers the permit side.

Being too close to the house is also the refusal reason the City's Certificate of Compliance page names for accessory residential buildings such as garages, sheds and gazebos. A Certificate of Compliance confirms that building locations comply with the Land Use Bylaw, based on a Real Property Report prepared by an Alberta land surveyor, and costs $189 per parcel. It does not review parcel coverage or building height.

## The 0.6 m apron at the lane

A driveway connecting a lane to a private garage must be at least 0.60 m long in the direction of travel, measured from the property line shared with the lane to the door of the garage. That is the garage apron. In practice, the garage door cannot sit right on the lane line.

If the garage faces a street instead, the rule is different. The bylaw calls for a driveway at least 6.0 m long, with a rectangular area of 6.0 m by 3.0 m. For a back-lane garage, which is the common Calgary layout, the 0.6 m figure is the one that applies.

## Eaves: 0.45 m and the non-vented soffit

Eaves are where a garage built close to the line gets caught. Two building-code rules the City applies near a side line are:

- Eave projections are not permitted within 0.45 m (1.5 ft) of a side property line shared with an adjacent property. Rain troughs are excluded from that measurement.
- A non-vented soffit, meaning the underside of the eave projection, is required for eaves within 1.2 m (3.9 ft) of a property line shared with an adjacent property.

The general bylaw rule for low-density districts lets eaves project up to 0.6 m into a side setback area. The 0.45 m code rule is the stricter of the two at the line. The zero-setback route is also tied to eaves: the wall qualifies only if there is no overhang of eaves onto the adjacent parcel.

Ask the builder to show you the eave and soffit detail on the drawings, and to confirm it meets both distances.

## Type X drywall within 0.6 m

For accessory buildings of 10 m² or more, the City requires 5/8 inch Type X drywall from floor to ceiling on all interior walls within 0.6 m (1.9 ft) of the side property line. At the framing inspection, the fire rating standards must be completed with the fire-rated materials installed.

This is a cost item the zero-setback plan carries. A garage built right at the line has a wall that fits that description along its full length. Ask each builder whether the quote includes it.

The City's pages do not give a rule for windows or doors in a garage wall near the line, so do not assume one. Airdrie and Okotoks publish their own. If you are building outside Calgary, see the [Airdrie](/garage-builders-airdrie/) and [Okotoks](/garage-builders-okotoks/) pages.

## Roof drainage and rights-of-way

All roof drainage from an accessory building must be discharged onto the parcel the building sits on. A garage near the line therefore needs gutters and downspouts that send water to your own lot, not onto the neighbour's side.

Permanent structures are not permitted in utility rights-of-way. The City includes footings, walls and eaves in that list. If a Maintenance Access Right-of-Way is registered on your title, its documents go in with the permit application. Check your title for any easement before you place the garage.

An older City list, updated in 2016, adds that provincial regulations prohibit building a garage over a gas line. The garage must be moved at least 0.3 m from the gas line or the line sleeved. Treat that as a dated reference and ask the City whether it still applies to your lot.

## Putting the numbers on the site plan

The City's garage permit checklist asks for property lines, streets and lane, the outline of the garage and house, and the distance from property lines and house to the proposed garage. It also asks for the length and location of the apron, and notes that a Real Property Report can serve as the site plan. The City says it may refuse an application if adequate information is not supplied within 90 days, so a site plan with missing distances is a risk.

If you plan to pour a parking pad for a garage you will build later, the City says to consider the location against the setback and design rules first, even though a pad itself needs no building permit.

A garage that meets every Land Use Bylaw rule needs no development permit, only a building permit and trade permits. The 2026 building permit for a new garage is $333.84. The [garage permit guide](/blog/calgary-garage-permit/) walks through who applies and what the City asks for.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How close can a garage be to the property line in Calgary?

The minimum setback for a detached garage is 0.6 m from a side or rear line, or 1.2 m where the line is shared with a street. It can be reduced to zero if the wall is maintenance-free with no eave overhang onto the neighbour, or if the neighbour grants a 1.5 m maintenance easement.

### How far does a detached garage have to be from the house?

At least 1.0 m between any façade of the garage and the house. The City says this includes second-floor cantilevers and bay windows. Closer than 1.0 m needs a development permit.

### Can I build a garage on the property line in Calgary?

Yes, under the conditions above. The wall must be maintenance-free with no eave overhang onto the neighbouring parcel, or you need the neighbour's registered easement. Roof water must stay on your parcel, and Type X drywall applies to interior walls within 0.6 m of the line.

### Can a detached garage go in the front setback area?

No. An accessory residential building must not be located in the actual front setback area. A laneless parcel gets one exception: a private garage may sit within the 3.0 m side setback, but not along the street side of a corner parcel.
