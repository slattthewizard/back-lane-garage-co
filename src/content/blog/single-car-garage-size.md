A single garage is the smallest building most Calgary builders will quote, and the size you pick is easy to get wrong. Twelve feet wide sounds fine until you try to open a truck door against a wall.

This page compares the single sizes Calgary builders actually advertise, with the prices they publish and the City rules that decide whether a single or a small double fits your lot. ## Single car garage dimensions builders advertise

Builders sell garages by outside dimensions, in feet. The sizes below are the single sizes that came up in published price lists. The metric and area figures are simple arithmetic on those outside dimensions.

| Nominal size | Metres (approx.) | Area | Who advertises it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12x20 | 3.7 m x 6.1 m | 240 sq ft (22.3 m2) | Corsac Construction |
| 14x20 | 4.3 m x 6.1 m | 280 sq ft (26.0 m2) | Venkor Group, Polskiy Building (Okotoks) |
| 14x22 | 4.3 m x 6.7 m | 308 sq ft (28.6 m2) | Precision Construction & Decora (roughly) |
| 14x24 | 4.3 m x 7.3 m | 336 sq ft (31.2 m2) | BVM Builders |

Outside size is not inside size. Two by four walls, sheathing and any interior finish take several inches off each dimension.

## What fits in a single garage

The City gives one useful reference. Its parking rules say a stall that touches two barriers must be at least 5.9 m x 3 m, and a stall with one barrier at least 5.9 m x 2.85 m. Garage walls count as barriers. The City adds that stalls cannot be in tandem. These are the City's stall minimums, not a promise that any particular vehicle fits comfortably, and the City page states that it has no legal status, so confirm the detail with the City.

Set that against the nominal sizes. A 12 foot width is about 3.7 m outside, so a 3 m stall on paper leaves little margin once walls are taken off. A 14 foot width is about 4.3 m outside, which gives roughly 1.3 m more than the minimum stall width. A 20 foot length is about 6.1 m outside, only a little more than the 5.9 m stall length, so wall thickness matters. A 22 or 24 foot length gives breathing room at the door and at the back wall.

That is arithmetic, not a vehicle test. Measure your own vehicle with mirrors, add room for the driver's door, and ask the builder for the inside dimensions of the size you are pricing.

A few practical points follow.

- 12x20 suits one smaller vehicle and little else. It is the cheapest published single, and it has the least room for a door swing, a bench or a snowblower.
- 14x20 gives two extra feet of width for about the same length. For many homeowners that is the better use of the money than adding length.
- 14x22 and 14x24 add depth. That depth is where a longer pickup, a bike rack or a storage wall goes.

## When a single makes more sense than a small double

Take the published starting prices and compare a single with the smallest double from the same firm. This is plain subtraction on advertised figures.

- Corsac: 12x20 at $18,500 and 20x20 at $25,900, a step of $7,400 plus GST.
- Venkor: 14x20 at $20,861 and 20x20 at $25,190, a step of $4,329, with GST not stated.
- Polskiy Building in Okotoks: 14x20 at $18,660 and 20x20 at $20,376, a step of $1,716 plus GST. That is an Okotoks price, not a Calgary one.

The step differs a lot by firm, and in some price lists it is smaller than people expect. Before you settle on a single to save money, get the 20x20 priced next to it. The post on [double garage size](/blog/double-garage-size/) covers what the larger sizes hold.

There are real reasons to stay with a single. The first is the lot.

- Narrow lots. The City's setbacks for an accessory building are 0.6 m from a side or rear property line, or 1.2 m where that line is shared with a street. There must be at least 1.0 m between the garage and the house. On a narrow lot, those distances can leave room for one bay and no more.
- Accessory building coverage. All accessory buildings on the parcel together must not exceed the lesser of the main house's building coverage or 75 m2 per dwelling unit. Buildings with a cumulative gross floor area of 10 m2 or less are left out of that count. A small house footprint or an existing large shed can cap the garage well below what you wanted.
- Parcel coverage. The City's page lists total parcel coverage limits by district, from 40 per cent in R-C1L to 45 per cent in R-C1 and R-C2, with other figures in other districts. A single uses less of that allowance than a double.
- Lane and apron. The garage door must sit at least 0.6 m from the lane property line. Lane geometry can shorten what the lot offers.

The second reason is use. If the garage will mostly house one car and the rest of the lot is worth more as yard, a single is a sound choice.

The third reason is budget. A bigger building adds slab, framing and roofing. Compare written quotes for both sizes before assuming the bigger building is a bargain.

## City rules that apply to every single garage

A single does not escape the rules. Confirm each point with the City of Calgary or your Town or County, because City pages carry a note that the information has no legal status.

A detached garage over 10 m2 (107 sq ft) needs a building permit. A 12x20 at about 22 m2 is well past that line. The City lists the new-garage building permit fee for 2026 at $333.84, which is $321 plus the 4 per cent Safety Codes Council fee. A new garage also always needs a separate electrical permit for lights, outlets and the overhead door.

A garage that meets every Land Use Bylaw rule needs a building permit and trade permits but no development permit. The City lists what does trigger one: a garage higher than 4.6 m, a wall-to-truss height over 3 m, a floor area over 75 m2 (807 sq ft), a garage larger than the existing house, or one closer than 1.0 m to the house. None of the single sizes above comes near the area limit, but height and distance to the house can still catch a tall door or a tight lot.

Slab size is not an issue. The City says a garage slab over 55 m2 (592 sq ft) without a concrete foundation 1.2 m below grade needs a slab designed by a professional engineer. Every single listed here is far under that. The [garage permit guide](/blog/calgary-garage-permit/) covers who applies and what the City asks for.

If the garage sits within 0.6 m of a side property line, the City requires 5/8 inch Type X drywall on interior walls within that distance. Ask whether the quote includes it if your garage will sit close to the fence.

## Choosing your size and getting quotes

Start from the vehicle and the one extra thing you would regret not having room for. Then price two sizes side by side.

If budget is the priority and you own one smaller vehicle, 12x20 is the cheapest published entry. If you want a bench and a door you can open fully, start at 14x20 or longer. If a small double is possible on your lot, price that too. Ask each builder to quote the same size, the same wall height, the same door and the same electrical service so the numbers line up.

Builders quote and contract directly with you. A builder who sells at your home and takes money before the work is done needs a prepaid contracting licence, which you can check on Service Alberta's [licence search](https://www.servicealberta.ca/find-if-business-is-licenced.cfm). A licence is not an endorsement of quality.

### Is a 12x20 garage big enough for a pickup truck?
